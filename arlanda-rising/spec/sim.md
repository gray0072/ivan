# Arlanda Rising — the rules as built, session by session

Part of the spec of `arlanda-rising`; the core rules and the index of these files are in [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md). Keep it in sync with the code like the rest of the spec. What `sim/` does today with its numbers in `constants.js` (session 1), and what sessions 3–6 added to the sim, its data, screens, map and cheats; the chapters they built are in [chapters.md](chapters.md), the browser of session 2 in [ui.md](ui.md).

## The rules as the sim has them (session 1)

What `sim/` does today, with its numbers in `constants.js`; later sessions add to it.

- **Steps.** Ops time runs in fixed steps of `OPS_STEP_MIN` (¼ game minute), plan time one day at a time (`sim/sim.js`), whatever the frame rate. The order of an ops step: scripted events, runways and snow, flights, the check-in queue, the tray, the running costs, the meters. A decision in plan time stops the clock until it is taken.
- **Flights** (`sim/schedule.js`, `sim/flights.js`) come from the stage's script as series (an airline's type, how many, between which times, the planned time on the ground); arrivals are early or late by the difficulty (`DIFFICULTY.jitter`). The "pick a stand" task appears `STAND_TASK_LEAD` minutes before landing. An arrival holds while the runway is closed and is sent elsewhere after `HOLD_MAX`; a departure waiting for a usable runway is cancelled after `DEP_WAIT_MAX`. Arrivals go before departures; one movement per `RWY_SEP` minutes with a tower, `RWY_SEP_NO_TOWER` without.
- **Turnaround** (`sim/turnaround.js`): passengers off, then the airline's own cleaning, catering and bags (the type's `svc`) beside the fuel, then boarding — not before `BOARD_LEAD` minutes ahead of departure and only once the flight's passengers are through check-in (or `BOARD_WAIT_MAX` after departure without the late ones). Snow on the apron makes every step slower. Fuel is the oil company's until a mission puts it in the player's hands (its truck is at the wing as the passengers are off: `tr.fuel` 'going' from `fuelAt` to `fuelEnd`); then a fuel truck must be sent and one truck serves one aircraft at a time: it sets off at `fuelFrom`, is at the wing at `fuelAt` (FUEL_DRIVE / 2 later) and the fuel flows from there to `fuelEnd`; the mobile charger the same with CHARGE_DRIVE.
- **The turnaround's bar and card** (`Turnaround.steps`, read by `render/traffic.js` and `ui/cards.js`, nothing in the sim reads it back): four steps, each *later* (not begun, nothing wrong), *held up* (it waits for something), *coming* (a vehicle on its way), *in progress* (a share and the minutes left) or *done*, worked out from the same fields and rules as the sim's step, so the bar, the card and the sim never disagree (`tools/turnaround-check.js`). **Passengers off** (blue): from in-block to `tr.off`; none for an aircraft here overnight. **Services** (green): the airline's cleaning and catering (`tr.off` → `tr.svc`) and, with the bags in the player's or a team's hands, the bags (the carts sent, BAG_CART_MIN at the hold) — as far as the slower of the two, held up while the carts are not sent. **Fuel** (yellow; *Charge* for an electric aircraft, *Hydrogen*): held up while the task waits (in the tray, a team's, the duty manager's — the card says who and when), for a free truck or charger; empty while the vehicle drives (shown pale as it comes), filled from the moment it is at the wing to `fuelEnd`. **Boarding** (violet): begins once the three are done and it is BOARD_LEAD before departure; held up when they are not done by the time its own minutes no longer fit before the departure (the card: *held up: waits for services, fuel — the departure at 10:30 will be late*); it runs for booked × BOARD_PER_PAX (× the snow and heat, × the biometric gates) but goes no further than the share of its passengers through the terminal, and is held up by the late ones until BOARD_WAIT_MAX past departure. **De-icing** (light blue): a small square after the bar from the moment boarding begins (held up until the de-icer comes, then filled from below); de-icing at a pad on the way out is only in the card. A step held up glows orange (pulsing) where it is not done. The flight's card shows the same bar, then each step with its colour's swatch (the legend), what it does (*the truck is coming · 2 min*, *45% · 6 min left*, *waiting for the baggage carts*) and, small, why and who (*a task in your tray*, *the team does it at 09:42*, *Fuel truck 2*, *by the oil company*, *the gate closes at 10:50*).
- **Delay and the airport's part.** A departure is on time up to `ONTIME_MIN` late. The airport's part of a delay is how much later it left than it could have with everything on time here — counted from its real arrival time, so a late inbound is not the airport's fault but waiting for a stand on the taxiway is. Penalties to the airlines and their mood follow the airport's part only; goals use either (`onTime`, `avgDelay`, `airportDelay`).
- **Check-in** (`sim/passengers.js`): each departure's passengers come between `PAX_ARRIVE_FIRST` and `PAX_ARRIVE_LAST` minutes before it and join one first-come queue served by the open desks; those who came before the stage began are through already. A queue of `CHECKIN_TASK_WAIT` minutes makes the "open a desk" task. The time left before boarding sets the café's takings. How many desks there are is limited by the halls' room (see "The build limits").
- **Snow** (`sim/runways.js`): the snowfall's millimetres build up on the runway and the apron. A runway's braking action is medium from 1 mm, poor from `SNOW_POOR_MM` (a landing then risks a close call), and it closes at `SNOW_CLOSE_MM`; the task appears at `SNOW_TASK_MM`. The plough convoy clears one runway at a time (slower with fewer than `PLOUGH_CONVOY` ploughs, the runway closed while it works); every `PLOUGH_CONVOY` ploughs more make another convoy, which sweeps another runway at the same time instead of waiting in the queue (`Runways.convoys`; no mission's own airport has two yet: 5–7 ploughs from 1983 on); the apron has its own small team beside them.
- **The build limits** (`Airport.cap` with `Airport.owned` and `Airport.full` in `sim/airport.js`; their words in `ui/text.js` — `capWhy` short on the buy menu's card, `capTip` in full under it at the limit, `buyTip` / `buyWhy` / `buyGain` for any small build — so the buy menu's cards and the decisions say the same): **Check-in desks:** the halls of the terminals standing have room for so many (`data/terminals.js` `desks`: the first terminal 12, the international terminal 16, the domestic terminal 10, Terminal 2 14, Terminal 3 4; the first terminal's hall stays with the kerb after 2017) — 12 in 1960, 28 in 1976, 38 in 1983, 42 in 1989, 56 from 1990; the desks counted are all of them (`S.airport.desks`: the terminals' own and those bought, the plan's not yet fitted too), so the button reads 🧳 Check-in desk 10/12 and at the room's end *Max: the halls are full* (a new terminal brings more room); the reference campaign has 4–10 in the first terminal, 22–42 later. In ops time the bar's − / + group says how many of them are open: *Open 2 of 4 desks*. **Security lanes:** the checkpoints of the terminals standing have room for so many (`data/terminals.js` `lanes`: the first terminal 4, the international terminal 16, the domestic terminal 8, Terminal 2 10, Terminal 3 3; `Airport.laneRoom`) — 41 from 1990, 37 once the first terminal is gone (2018); every lane counts (`S.airport.lanes`: a mission's and those bought), *Max: the security checkpoints are full*; the reference campaign has 10–33 (8-10's 33 of 37). The bar's *Lanes 8/12* is the lanes open of those that can open now (`Terminal.lanesUp`: a lane needs its screeners, so a security team laid off or furloughed opens fewer), its tooltip says so. **Passport booths:** `BOOTH_ROOM` = 12 in the passport hall (6 come with it; the campaign has up to 10). Before, lanes and booths had no limit, and the card counted only those bought (×3 for an airport with 15). **Remote stands:** `REMOTE_STANDS_MAX` = 4, their row's places (see "The map as built" in [world.md](world.md)), 🅿️ Remote stand 2/4, *Max: 4 remote stands*; they are named R1–R4 (the aprons' stands keep the numbers; before, a remote stand took the next number, so the reference's of 1997 was a second stand 17 and got jet bridges with Pier F's stand 17 — a contact stand from then on); a save of an older version with more is cut to four at a mission's start, the last ones bought paid back (`Airport.fitRemote`). A decision that would build past a limit builds what fits (its line says *room for 1 more* or the limit's reason, and the price of what is built): 6-3's *Two new remote stands* builds one for an airport with three. The reference keeps its three remote stands (1962, 1963, 1997) and puts 6-3's royal guests at the terminal instead (it built two before, a fifth and a sixth stand); the campaign's ops stages are chaotic — a stand more or less moves every later random draw — so with that 8-8's star for the airport's delay is ≤ 4 min (it was ≤ 3: the reference's Easy run got 3.8 once). **Car parks** have no count of their own: see "Car parks" below. **The vehicles' limits** (shown on the build buttons as ⛽ Fuel truck 3/6 and in the vehicles' and the fire station's cards as "3 of 6 worth having"): past them one more does nothing, so the button is disabled with the reason. **Fuel trucks:** one per stand (`FUEL_TRUCKS_PER_STAND`) — an aircraft takes one truck at a time, so with a truck for every stand none ever waits (in the reference campaign 4 stands in 1960, 9–10 with the jet apron in 1965–76, 13–17 in 1983–99, 21–26 in 2003–24, 32 in 2040; the missions bring 1–6 trucks). **De-icing trucks:** the same, one per stand (`DEICERS_PER_STAND`). **Snowploughs:** a full convoy for each runway, `PLOUGH_CONVOY` × runways — 4 with runway 1, 8 with runway 2 (1970s), 12 with runway 3 (2003); the apron's team needs none of its own. **Crash tenders:** `FIRE_CAT_MAX − FIRE_CAT_BASE` = 3, since each raises the fire category by one from 7 to the top, 10. The count takes the buys of plan time not yet delivered; the owner's vehicles a mission brings are never taken away. **One is enough, or a few** (a kind's own `max` in `data/buildings.js`): a backup generator, the airport coach stops, the electric apron vehicles, the security operations centre, the hydrogen depot and the night train platform one each (*Max: one is enough*), two solar parks (the green rating counts two), five cargo stands (their places on the cargo apron). **Charging stands:** one on each stand near the terminal up to size code D (`Airport.chargeable`), *Max: every stand that fits has one*. The reference campaign stays under every limit but two: 1-11 buys two ploughs for a full convoy, which a chain already has from 1-7, and 3-11 a backup generator, which a chain has from 3-3's computer room (its backup power), so in the chain those are refused (the stars and the meters stay the same; the money from 1-11 on is a few MSEK better, from 3-11 on 6 MSEK more). Heavy snowfall also hides the runway like fog (`FOG_VIS_MIN`, `FOG_VIS_APPROACH` with approach lights).
- **The dark and the noise.** The sun follows 59.65° N and the date; a runway without lights takes no movement in civil darkness. When the wind is under `CALM_WIND` the player picks the direction: landing on 01 comes in over Märsta, taking off on 19 climbs out over it (with runway 3 the take-offs on 19L climb out over Upplands Väsby); a movement over a town makes `NOISE_ARR_TOWN` (a landing) or `NOISE_DEP_TOWN` (a take-off, louder), one over the forest `NOISE_FOREST`, a jet ×1.5, at night ×`NOISE_NIGHT_X`, and each pulls the Neighbours toward their base less `NEIGHBOURS_NOISE_K` × the noise. So 01 is the quieter direction unless the landings due outnumber the take-offs by more than about 2.4 to 1 (an arrivals wave): with as many of each it is about 40 % less noise. **Shown:** with the wind blowing the bar says *🛫 Runway 19 · the wind decides* (no buttons); when calm it has two buttons, **01** *Landings over Märsta* and **19** *Take-offs over Märsta* (*… over Upplands Väsby* with runway 3), and over them *🏡 01 is quieter now* from the flights due in the next `NOISE_AHEAD_MIN` minutes (`Runways.noiseAhead`: the landings not yet down and the departures due, weighed as the sim weighs them; nothing when both directions are the same). Kaj says so once the wind falls calm with the noisier direction in use (hint `calm`). The choice stays the player's (`S.airport.prefDir`, '19' at the start).
- **The tray's other hands** (`sim/tasks.js`): a task not done by its deadline, or one that finds the tray full, goes to the duty manager, who does it `DUTY_LATE` minutes later and picks the right stand only `MANUAL_SUMMARY_EFF` of the time (a wrong one costs `WRONG_STAND_DELAY`). A kind of task the mission does not put in the player's hands is done at once by someone else; a domain handed to people does its tasks after its level's delay (`data/domains.js`).
- **Meters** (`sim/meters.js`) move as moving averages of each departure's result (`METER_EMA`): the waits, the delay, walking from a remote stand, missed passengers; noise pulls the Neighbours toward their base minus the noise; Safety drops at a close call and recovers slowly to the airport's base.
- **Plan time** (`sim/plan.js`, `sim/construction.js`): a stage of a project starts when the stages before it are done and a crew is free; it is paid day by day as it progresses (a fast pace: `PACE`), at half speed (`WINTER_WORK`) in December–February unless a mod says otherwise. A mod whose only effect is that (`noWinter`: the heated winter tents; T4–T5's *Work at night too*) is offered in the plan panel only while it can matter — the project is not finished and a day of plan time left in the mission (the rest of this stage, then the plan stages after it on their dates: `Construction.winterAhead`) falls in December–February — with what it does under it (*Work goes on at full speed in December–February (otherwise half speed)*, also the button's tooltip); otherwise the button is not there and `Actions.mod` refuses it (`Construction.modCannot`: `done`, `noWinter`), so it is never money thrown away. One already bought stays shown with ✓. An optional stage has its own effect. A stage with `endEarly` ends once its goal is met and no work is still going on. The open airport's traffic runs in summary (`sim/summary.js`): flights a day from the airlines' routes, fees, shops and parking, less `SUMMARY_LOSS` for every domain still done by hand.
- **The airport's own shops take cells in the terminals** (`sim/airport.js`, the numbers in `data/buildings.js` and `data/terminals.js`): each terminal standing brings its **cells** — the first terminal 6, the international terminal 8, the domestic terminal 4, Terminal 2 4, Terminal 3 2 (Sky City and the kerb none: they have only the brands' units) — and each own shop takes some: a **kiosk 1** (0.6 MSEK), a **café 2** (2.5 MSEK), a **restaurant 3** (8 MSEK, from 1965), a **duty-free shop 4** (30 MSEK, from 1976). A shop bought goes into the first terminal in `TERMINAL_ORDER` with enough cells free (the duty-free shop into the international terminal first: its `term`) and keeps them while it is fitted in plan time (`S.plan.buys[].term`); a shop the master plan or a mission gives goes the same way (`it.term`; one of an older save without one is placed at once in the first terminal with room, `Airport.shopPlaces`). A shop no terminal has the cells for cannot be bought: the build button says *No room: needs 2 cells*. Each shop brings its `shop` share of SHOP_SPEND per departing passenger, airport-wide, with no other cap than the cells (the old cap of three shares, SHOP_MAX, is gone: the reference campaign's kiosk, café, duty free and restaurant of 1988 earn 3.3 shares instead of 3, and the second duty-free shop 4-4 builds — refused before, the room full — is built). **Selling:** in plan time with the airport open (where shops are bought) a shop is sold back for SHOP_SELL_BACK (half) of its price from its terminal's card (the line `sold` in the books), to free its cells for another — two kiosks out make room for a café. `Actions.sell(id or kind)` does it for a strategy. The cells are apart from the brands' rented units of chapter 4 on (`sim/terminal.js`): the own shops are the airport's from the first kiosk, the brands rent their units; the cells may be rented out one day. **Seen:** with the roofs on, an awning in the shop's colour (`PAL.shop`) on its terminal's landside wall, as long as its cells, never a building of its own; with the roofs off, each shop in its cells in the terminal's shop column before the brands' units (its colour, its sign and its emoji, a counter, tables or shelves, a seam between its cells; one being fitted behind a striped barrier; each free cell an empty bay), so the shops and the cells can be counted; the build toolbar shows the price and the cells (*☕ Café ×1 · 2.5 M · 2 cells · 25 days*) and *🏪 Shop cells 3 of 6 free*; the terminal's card lists *Shops: 4 / 6 cells* with each shop, its cells and a **Sell +1.3 M** button (a second tap sells), the free cells, and the shops being fitted. Small builds still being fitted when a plan stage ends are finished after it (they were lost before).
- **Car parks** (`sim/airport.js`: `places`, `parkingNeed`, `parkingShare`, `parkingMonth`, `parkingGain`): the drivers among a day's departing passengers need `PARK_PLACES_PER_PAX` (0.2) places each (`Invest.paxDay`: the last days in summary, or the routes before any), less the share the airport coaches (`BUS_PARKING`, from 1989) and the train (`TRAIN_PARKING`, from November 1999) take away — about 130 places in 1960, 600 in 1976, 1 200 in 1988, 700–1 100 with the coaches and the train in 1990–2024, 1 400 at a peak of 2040. A car park has its places (`data/buildings.js` `places`: the small one 300, the big one 1 200); the car parks take PARKING_PER_PAX of every departing passenger × the share of the drivers they have room for (places ÷ need, at most all) × the drivers left after the coaches and the train — in ops time at check-in, in plan time in summary. So a car park earns only while the traffic needs its places: one more than the need earns nothing and cannot be bought (*Enough: one more would earn nothing*) until the traffic grows; the small one is enough in the 1960s, the big one in the 1980s. **Shown:** the bar of plan time *🚗 Car parks 300 places, 700 needed* (green once covered; its tooltip what they take a month), each car park button what it would add a month at today's traffic (*6 M · 20 days · +45 k a month*), the decisions' lines its payback or the reason it is not built, the car park's card the places, the share of the drivers and the parking fees a month, the Finance screen each one's part of the takings and the what-ifs (`Invest.building`: an owned one its places' part, a new one its `parkingGain`). Before, a car park took a fixed share of the drivers whatever the traffic (the small one 40 %, the big one all), and the train took nothing in the sim (only in the estimates): now it takes its third, about 1–3 MSEK a month less from 2000 on in the reference campaign.
- **Between missions** the calendar jumps in summary; the owner builds the historical projects a mission `requires` (free), and a campaign that comes in below zero gets the owner's loan for the gap, so it never gets stuck.

## People, systems, chapters 2–3, Free Play and the cloud (session 3)

What session 3 added; its numbers are in `constants.js` (the people, the systems, the airfield), the data in `data/domains.js`, `data/people.js`, `data/masterplan.js`, `data/missions/ch2.js` and `ch3.js`.

- **The ladder** (`data/domains.js`, `sim/tasks.js`, `sim/systems.js`): six domains — stands, turnaround (fuel), check-in, snow, baggage (bags) and information (announcements); each level has its staff, price, monthly cost, delay and share of good choices, a system (level 2) also its IT jobs, mean time between failures, fix time and days to install. A domain is part of the game once a mission opens it (`setup.opens`), so chapter 1's worlds are untouched. A task goes to the level that works now: a system (`by: 'system'`), a team (its skill and its manager change its delay and choices; when it is short or on strike, the task falls to the player's tray — `st.short`), or the player's hands. A system that fails hands its work to the team kept beside it (worse with a low readiness: delay × (2 − readiness)) or, laid off, to the tray. The Office may buy a level once a mission has unlocked it (`S.unlock`, `setup.unlock`); systems need the computer room (the belts the baggage hall).
- **Systems** (`sim/systems.js`): random failures in ops time at the system's MTBF × the IT factor (too few IT people: more often, longer), scripted ones (`events: fail`), a power cut (every system without a backup generator); the readiness falls READINESS_DECAY a month without a drill (DRILL_COST); in summary a system loses its share of SUMMARY_LOSS by its downtime and fallback. Buying: `keep` (retrain the team, RETRAIN_COST a job; its wages go on; morale up) or `layoff` (every team's morale −LAYOFF_MORALE, the unions' mood down, Neighbours −3, no fallback).
- **People** (`sim/people.js`): a department per domain with a team (and IT): morale (drifts to MORALE_BASE + the manager + a wage deal's fading bonus − fatigue), fatigue (overtime; halves every month), skill (SKILL_START, +SKILL_MONTH, a course +TRAIN_SKILL; check-in desks work at 0.8 + 0.4 × skill, ×CHECKIN_SYSTEM_X with the computers), absence (ABSENCE_BASE × the flu season FLU × morale and fatigue × the difficulty, + SUMMER_AWAY in July unless spread, − the temps); a team's capacity is 1 − (absence − ABSENCE_SLACK) × SHORT_K, STRIKE_CAPACITY on strike, 1 with overtime (OVERTIME_STAGE of a day's wages × OVERTIME_X, +OVERTIME_FATIGUE). Hiring takes HIRE_DAYS in plan time (CLEARANCE_DAYS where a department needs a clearance — none before chapter 5), at once between missions. The Staff meter is the departments' morale weighted by their jobs.
- **Unions**: a mission's wage round is a decision (`kind: 'union'`): accept (WAGE_DEMAND), a counter-offer the union takes when 0.15 + 0.6 × offer / demand + (mood − 50) / 100 + the manager's `strike` perk ≥ 0.6 (deterministic, its likely outcome shown), the mediator (¾ of the demand), refuse — a strike notice, the strike STRIKE_NOTICE_DAYS later for STRIKE_DAYS, ending with the demand met. A deal calls a notice off. A round that falls due between missions is settled at WAGE_DRIFT (no inflation in the game).
- **Managers** (`data/people.js`): ten fictional people for five departments, each with a salary, two strengths and a flaw as numbers the sim reads (`perk`); candidates in pairs (a decision, the People screen, Free Play's offers); their mood, a raise asked every MANAGER_RAISE_MONTHS (answered in People, or "not now" at the next mission), poaching (MANAGER_POACH a month under 40) and retiring (MANAGER_YEARS).
- **The airfield**: the fire category (FIRE_CAT_BASE with the fire station — the master plan's project `fireStation`, built with the first terminal in 1-4, its price taking in its first crash tenders and their crews — + a crash tender bought each, up to FIRE_CAT_MAX; before the station stands, the building site's fire watch by the runway gives FIRE_CAT_SITE, enough for 1-2's and 1-3's training Caravelles and shown as such in the Office and the Safety analytics (`Airport.siteFireWatch`); an arrival needing more is sent elsewhere unless it has a `waiver`), emergencies (`events: emergency`: the next arrival lands first, the runway closed EMERGENCY_CLOSE, longer and a close call with too low a category), runway 2 (08/26, the AIP's ends; used when runway 1 cannot take movements: a crosswind over CROSSWIND_MAX, snow, a convoy), the instrument landing system (FOG_VIS_ILS, LVP_SEP_X), bags (BAG_CART_MIN; boarding waits; a wrong cart loses LOST_BAG_SHARE), announcements (a flight ANNOUNCE_LEAD minutes late must be told within ANNOUNCE_OK, or ANNOUNCE_MOOD off the Passengers).
- **New goals**: level, manager, noStrike, lostBags, unannounced, fireCat, systems, handsOff, handTasks, lastMonth (the last month's result without one-off sums: the payback of an investment).
- **Free Play** (`sim/freeplay.js`): opens with 2-11; three eras (1963 always, 1976 after 2-11, 1990 after 3-11), each built by the owner; then rounds made from the airport (a seed and the round's number): a plan stage of FREE_PLAN_DAYS with two offers of different groups (an airline's slots, a shop or a car park, a manager pair, a team or a system, a wage round, a crash tender) and an ops peak flown from the airlines' routes, its goal FREE_GOAL_ONTIME on time; no stars. Kept in `arlandaRising.freeplay.v1`; a round being played is in the mission autosave (its definition in `S.mission.def`). Between rounds the Office works on Free Play's airport.
- **Screens**: the People screen and the Ops Center (from the Office, keys P and O, at once; during play to look only, the game paused), the plan panel's Teams and systems, the toolbar's short teams with Overtime and the systems down, the decision card with the managers' lines and the union's mood, "Meanwhile…" with the people's news, the stage card with who is away or on strike, the results with the teams' and systems' work, the exams' front pages (`paper`), Kaj's new hints, the flight card's bags and emergency, the Free Play screen and its results.
- **The map**: runway 2 with its taxiway, the jet apron, the domestic apron, the international terminal (hall and pier), the domestic terminal, the baggage hall, the computer room, the generator, crash tenders, the airport coaches, the localizer; stands placed by their project (`at`); aircraft on runway 2 land, roll out and taxi by its own way; the DC-9, 727, 747, DC-10, F28 and MD-81 drawn to scale.
- **Cheats**: Shift+2 and Shift+3 the chapter's first mission (with the owner's arrangement of its year), Shift+K a strike of the first team, Shift+F a failure of the first system.

## The terminals, brands, the economy screens, chapter 4 and Challenge Days (session 4)

What session 4 added; its numbers are in `constants.js` (the terminal's flow, brands and ground transport; airlines, incentives, rivals and offers; Challenge Days), the data in `data/terminals.js`, `data/brands.js`, `data/airlines.js`, `data/masterplan.js`, `data/missions/ch4.js` and `data/challenges.js`. Nothing of it runs before a chapter 4 mission turns it on (`setup.openSkies`, `units`, `lanes`, `rivals`), so chapters 1–3 play exactly as before (`tools/missions-check.js` gives the same numbers).

- **The terminals' insides** (`sim/terminal.js`): a departure goes through a terminal by its airline (`S.airlines[code].term`, Terminal 2 for SAS domestic from 1990) or by domestic / international. Each terminal has **shop units** of three sizes (kiosk, unit, big; `data/terminals.js`), apart from the cells of the airport's own shops, and the kerb has desks; `setup.units` makes them for every terminal standing (the first terminal has none once the international and domestic ones stand). A **brand** (`data/brands.js`: 34 real chains, each from its checked first year in Sweden, with its rent, its spend per passenger, whom it draws — everybody, business, holiday, families in FAMILY_MONTHS, international only — and the Passengers points it gives) opens in a unit that fits it for the fit-out (UNIT_FIT), pays its rent every month (the line `rent`) and its share of what each departing passenger of that terminal spends at full free time airside (× SHOP_SPEND; a second brand of a kind shares with the first, BRAND_SAME_KIND). A brand is never in one terminal twice.
- **Security** (from chapter 4, `S.airport.lanes`): after check-in the passengers queue for the lanes (LANE_RATE each, every lane open, LANE_COST_HOUR in ops time, LANE_MONTH in summary); the free time for the shops is counted when they come through, so a long queue costs shop money, and boarding waits for them like for check-in. A lane is a small build (`lane`); an ops event `lanesShut` closes some for the stage. The Passengers meter counts both waits.
- **Ground transport:** the coaches (as before), the car parks, and **Arlanda Express** (`arlandaExpress`: the tunnel, the line, Arlanda South and North; the deal `own` — the airport builds it, the owner's grant pays half, TRAIN_TICKET per train passenger — or `concession` — a partner builds it with its own money (`finance: 'partner'`), TRAIN_STATION_FEE). It runs from TRAIN_FIRST_DAY (24 November 1999) whenever it was built: TRAIN_SHARE of the departing passengers, TRAIN_PARKING of the car parks' takings gone, TRAIN_PAX on the Passengers meter, TRAIN_NEIGHBOURS on the Neighbours' base.
