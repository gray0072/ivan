# Arlanda Rising — the world: Arlanda

Part of the spec of `arlanda-rising`; the core rules and the index of these files are in [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md). Keep it in sync with the code like the rest of the spec. **Binding for every change to the map, the drawings, the vehicles, a name, a brand or a text the player sees.**

The game must **look and feel like Arlanda** in every session. Before drawing or placing any landmark, check it against the sources at the end of this section. Facts that are shown to the player (the Archive, mission briefings, "Real Arlanda" numbers, which airline flew when) are checked before they ship and noted in `data/archive.js` / `data/airlines.js` with their source.

## Geography and layout

- **Setting:** Sigtuna municipality, about 40 km north of Stockholm. Flat land of **pine and spruce forest with birch edges, granite outcrops, small lakes** (Halmsjön lies north of runway 3), fields, red wooden farmhouses (Falu red). **Märsta** town to the south-west, the **E4** motorway to the west, the **East Coast railway** (Ostkustbanan) to the east, road 273 through the airport area.
- **Runways** (real, final state): **runway 1 = 01L/19R, 3 300 m** (the long one); **runway 3 = 01R/19L, 2 500 m**, parallel, opened 2003, away from the terminals and reached over **taxiway bridges**, with its own remote **de-icing area** near it; **runway 2 = 08/26, 2 500 m**, the crosswind runway. Runways 1 and 3 can be used independently. High-speed exits on all but 08/26. *Exact positions, the taxiway system and the terminal area's place between them are taken from the official aerodrome chart (Swedish AIP, ESSA AD 2) before the map is drawn — not guessed.* In code (`data/layout.js`): the three runways' real ends from OurAirports' `runways.csv` (the AIP's data): runway 1 from 59.637298° N 17.913200° E (01L) to 59.666401° N 17.923800° E (19R), 3 301 × 45 m, true heading 10°; runway 3 about 2 km east of it, runway 2 to the north-east. The map's origin is the 01L threshold, x east and y north in metres. The first apron, its stands, the first terminal and tower, the 1960s taxiways and the road are the game's drawing in the right place (east of runway 1, where the terminals still are), marked to be checked against a chart of the era; so are Halmsjön's outline and place.
- **Terminals** (final state): **Terminal 5** (international, piers **D, E, F**; **Arlanda North / Pier F** opened 2003; the former **Terminal 4** domestic terminal joined to it airside in 2022), **Terminal 2**, **Terminal 3** (regional), **Sky City** between the terminals (shops, hotel, the station hall). The original 1960s terminal and later ones appear in their era; what became of each is checked in the sources. **Names by year, as at the real Arlanda** (`data/terminals.js` `names`, `Terminal.name`; every card, tab, list and offer shows the name of the year): the international terminal (1976, *Arlanda International*) is **Terminal 5** from 1993; the domestic terminal (1983) is **Domestic 1** (Inrikes 1) from 1990, **Terminal 4** from 1993 and **Terminal 5 · Pier C** from 2025, when Terminal 4 was joined to Terminal 5; the terminal of 1990 opens as **Domestic 2** (Inrikes 2) and is **Terminal 2** from 1993 (the numbering 2–5: sv.wikipedia ties it to the rebuilding of 1992, en.wikipedia gives 1993; 1 was kept for a terminal south of Terminal 2 never built). South to north they stand as in reality: Terminal 2, Terminal 3, the domestic terminal (Terminal 4), Sky City across the road, Terminal 5 with Pier F.
- **Terminal 3** (`terminal3`, `t3`): the owner builds it in August 1989 (before chapter 3's exam; no mission of its own, `requires` from then on) — a low hall (80 × 21 m, 6 m high) in the gap between Terminal 2 and the domestic terminal, its kerb in line with theirs, 2 check-in desks, 8 jobs, 2 cells for the own shops, a unit and a kiosk for the brands. A **domestic flight by propeller** (`AIRCRAFT[type].engines === 'prop'`: SAS's Fokker 50s of the 1990s and 2000s, the electric commuters of the 2030s) goes through it while it stands and is open (`Terminal.of(code, intl, type)`), else through the domestic terminal or Terminal 5. Named *The regional terminal* until 1993 (its name before the numbering was not found), **Terminal 3** from 1993 and **Terminal 2 · gates 51–59** from 2027. In 2020 (7-1) *Close Terminals 2, 3 and 4* mothballs it with the others; chapter 8 (2030) opens it again (`setup.unmothball`; the real one reopened in October 2024, closed as a terminal in September 2026 and its gates joined to Terminal 2 at the end of 2026). Free Play's 2024 airport has it mothballed (until October).
- **The first terminal torn down** (`oldHall`, 2017): the real first international hall of 1962 — the domestic hall until 1983, then offices and storage — was torn down in 2017 to make room for the longer Pier F (Flygtorget). In the game the owner tears it down before the exam of 2018 (`requires` from then on, no mission of its own): its item `terminal` goes and `S.airport.t1Gone` is set (`Airport.razeFirstTerminal`); the kerb and the check-in desks' button stay (`Terminal.built('kerb')`); its brands' units close; its own shops move into the other terminals' free cells, one at a time, and one with no room anywhere is sold back for SHOP_SELL_BACK of its price. The building, its waiting room and their cards are gone; the plot is grass, the road in still ends at the old door, the baggage hall, the computer room and the first tower stay. The passengers of an aircraft walk to the nearest terminal or pier standing (`render/traffic.js` walkTo) instead of the first terminal.
- **Towers:** the **old control tower** (1960s) and the **new tower of 2001 by Wingårdh** — a slender concrete sculpture (European Award for Excellence in Concrete 2002; heights given differently by sources, about 74 m to the balcony and about 83 m in all — check before using).
- **Rail:** **Arlanda Express** (opened 1999, a concession) in a tunnel with underground stations (Arlanda South, Arlanda North) and **Arlanda C** for SJ, SL commuter and Upptåget trains. The trains in their real livery (check the current one).
- **Other landmarks to place in their era:** car park houses, the cargo area, fire stations, the de-icing pads, the hotels, the fuel farm, the snow-clearing depot, the jumbo-jet hostel at the entrance road (**Jumbo Stay**, a Boeing 747 turned into a hostel — check whether it is still there; if closed, it appears only in its years).
- **The map as built — the plan and its rules.** Everything has its place in `data/layout.js` (metres, x east, y north from the 01L threshold); `docs/layout.svg` is its plan from above with every era at once (north up, a 100 m grid, the biggest aircraft each stand takes in blue dashes), written by `node arlanda-rising/tools/layout-check.js --svg arlanda-rising/docs/layout.svg` — **look at it first when anything on the map seems out of place, and regenerate it after moving anything.** The rules the checker enforces: no building on another building, an apron, a stand, a road, the railway or a taxiway; no road or railway across an apron, a stand, a runway or a taxiway except at the two taxiway bridges (the rail tunnel is under the ground); no taxiway through a building; the biggest aircraft of a stand's code clear of every building, road, runway and the next stand's aircraft. **The remote stands** the player buys (at most `REMOTE_STANDS_MAX` = 4, code C: up to a Boeing 737 or an A321) stand in a row of their own south-west of the first apron (`LAYOUT.remote`: x 680, y 1075, 1020, 965, 910, nose east, 55 m apart), each on its own pad, their paved ways running north along x 605 onto the first apron's south-west corner — west of the domestic apron (1983) and Terminal 2's (1990), so no apron, building or lead-in line of any later era ever reaches them and they stay where they are to 2040; the checker forbids a remote stand's pad, aircraft or way on any apron (the way may join the first) or on another stand's aircraft. Before, six places were used in turn (a seventh stand on the first): two of them lay under the domestic apron in front of its stands 9–11, one on the jet apron, and one's way ran through two others' aircraft. The landside is one line north–south: the road in from the E4 under the taxiway bridge, past Cargo City (its halls on the road, its stands nose east to them on the cargo apron, reached from taxiway 3), along the kerb of Terminal 2 and the domestic terminal, past the bus stop, then in a curve west onto the first terminal's kerb, its door in the middle of its east face, in every year; from the international terminal's year on the landside road forks off it just before that curve (a Y: the road in bends left to the first terminal, the landside road bends gently right and runs on north) to the international terminal and Pier F, with the car parks, Sky City, the hotel, the offices and the security operations centre east of it. The roads' verges are all painted before any road's carriageway (render/ground.js), so a fork shows no verge across the road. **A terminal's landside face (east) is its kerb and its door: no building stands on the 40 m in front of it** (`tools/layout-check.js` checks it, and that the road in ends at the first terminal's door); the baggage hall (1973) is joined to the first terminal's south end, as wide as the terminal, its west face on the apron's edge where the baggage trains come in. Airside lies west of the terminals: the aprons in a row from Terminal 2 to the second arm, the stands nose east to their piers (the satellite's east side nose west, reached round its south end); taxiway 2 leaves the jet apron's north-west corner and runs along the aprons' west edge and east round the arm's north end (y 2450, north of runway 2's line), never across an apron, then turns right in a curve of 40 m into a straight entry square to runway 2 from its north side, onto its centreline 12 m inside the 08 end, with the holding line across it 60 m from the centreline (`runway2.hold`); the drawn taxiway ends flush with the runway's edge. The checker fails a turn of taxiway 2 sharper than a right angle and an entry that is not square to the runway, ends off its centreline or away from its 08 end. Runway 2's strip runs 60 m past each end with square ends, and the forest is cleared to them (render/map.js, not a round clearing round the ends). The international terminal is one building: its low pier along the gates (west) is drawn before its tall departure hall (east), which hides the pier's east wall.
- **The vehicles' places west of the aprons** (`data/layout.js`, between taxiway 1's strip and the aprons, south to north): the departures' link to the first apron; the **ploughs' standing** (`snowDepot`: two rows of six nose south on asphalt, the first row filled first); the crash tenders' bays in front of the **fire station**; the **fuel trucks' yard** (`fuelYard`) east of the fire station, behind the ploughs as the map is seen — a concrete pad with white bays, a red-edged shelter on posts over its back row, two rows of twelve trucks nose south (the open front row first, the thirteenth and later under the shelter) and an aisle south of them, with its own lane east onto the jet apron's west edge (`gate`). Until the jet apron's paving begins (1965) the trucks wait by the first apron's north edge (`fuelDepot`), where the jet apron is laid; from then on in the yard (`AirportArt.fuelYardOn` / `fuelSpot`). From the yard a truck drives out of its bay to the aisle, along the lane to the gate, then on the apron as before: along the first apron's north edge to a stand on the first apron or south of it, or straight along the lane behind the stands to one on the jet apron or north of it, up to the wing and back the same way. The forest is cleared (`clear.ploughs`, `clear.yard`, drawn by render/map.js `clearings`) from the strip's clearing to the aprons round the ploughs and the tenders' bays once there is a plough or a fire station, and round the fire station and the yard once the yard is in use; the yard is part of the airside fence. `tools/layout-check.js` checks the yard, its lane and the ploughs' rows.
- **The E4 and its junction.** The E4 runs north–south past the map's edge at both ends (on towards Stockholm and Uppsala), so it never stops in the forest. Where the road in meets it (`e4Exit` in `data/layout.js`) the E4 goes **over** the road on a bridge: a deck 8 m up, 17 m to each side of the road, on grass embankments 140 m long, with parapets and an abutment wall; the road goes on under it west to Märsta and four slip roads (a diamond) join the two, the grass between them cleared of trees. It is drawn once the road in is finished (render/ground.js); while the road is being built the gravel road ends under the E4.
- **The airside fence** (`render/fence.js`) is worked out from what is built or being built, never drawn by hand: the runways with their strips (140 m to each side, 200 m past the ends), the taxiways (40 m to each side), the aprons and the remote stands (20 and 15 m round), the de-icing pads, the fire station and the hydrogen depot are laid on a 10 m grid, the gaps between them up to 500 m wide are fenced in with them, then the landside is cut away again — the belt east of the terminal line (`fence.belt`), 25 m round every road, 21 m round the railway, the car parks, Sky City, the offices, Cargo City's yard, the solar parks, and 120 m of ground in front of each terminal's landside face — except over the two taxiway bridges, where the fence follows the bridge's sides across the road and the railway. The buildings on the terminal line (`fence.line`: the terminals, the baggage and sorting halls, Cargo City) and the piers are part of it: along their walls no fence is drawn, and a run that reaches one ends at its wall (`tools/layout-check.js` fails a run that ends in the open). The first tower stands north of the waiting room, a step off the apron's edge, clear of the fence that runs from the waiting room's corner round the apron. Once the first terminal is torn down (2017) its plot, its waiting room's and the ground in front stay landside (`cuts`): the fence runs 10 m inside the old west face, off the apron's edge, from the baggage hall to the computer room. So the road in, the kerb and the car parks always stay outside, runway 3 is fenced in with the corridor of taxiway 3, and the fence grows with every apron, pier, runway or remote stand. It appears when the site project is finished, is worked out again only when the set of parts changes (about 0.1 s) and is drawn as posts every 14 m with a 3 m mesh, no trees on its line. `tools/layout-check.js` checks it with everything built and in six eras (1960, 1976, 1990, 2003, 2018 with the first terminal gone, 2040 with the satellite: across no road, runway, taxiway, apron or building of the era, every open run ending at a wall) and draws it on the plan.

## Real names and brands

The game uses **real names, liveries and logos**, drawn as **simplified pictures** (procedural canvas drawings or small SVGs made for the game, never copied image files), as `world-aviation` does.

- **The airport and the authorities:** Arlanda is run by **Luftfartsverket (LFV)** until 2010 and by **Swedavia** from 2010 (the owner changes in chapter 6, with Swedavia's colours on signs and vehicles from then on); air traffic control by LFV; **Transportstyrelsen** (the safety authority) in inspections; **Polisen** at the border, **Tullverket** at customs.
- **Airlines:** the real airlines of each era, in their real colours and emblems of that era (see "Airlines" in [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md)).
- **Ground transport:** **Arlanda Express**, **Flygbussarna**, **SL** commuter trains (from 2012) and buses, **SJ**, **Upptåget**, taxis (**Taxi Stockholm**, **Taxi Kurir**, **Sverigetaxi**), car rental (**Hertz**, **Avis**, **Europcar**, **Sixt**).
- **Terminal shops, cafés and restaurants** — many, and ones a Swedish family knows at a glance; recognisable places are what make the terminal feel real. The player picks them for the shop units (see "The master plan" in [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md)), from a catalogue of real chains, each available **from the year it existed in Sweden** (no Joe & The Juice in 1975). A first list, each to be checked (the exact name, logo and colours, the first year in Sweden; marked when it really was at Arlanda):
  - **Cafés and juice:** **Joe & The Juice**, **Espresso House**, **Waynes Coffee**, **Starbucks**, **Pressbyrån** (coffee and a bun), **7-Eleven**, **Gateau**, **Le Pain Français**.
  - **Fast and casual food:** **Hawaiian Poké** (check the Swedish chain's exact name), **Sushi Yama**, **Max**, **Bastard Burgers**, **Burger King**, **McDonald's**, **Sibylla** (hot dogs), **Vapiano**, **Pinchos**, **O'Learys** (sports bar), **Pizza Hut**.
  - **Shops:** the duty-free operator of the era, **Pressbyrån** (papers, sweets), **Akademibokhandeln** (books), **Clas Ohlson**, **Kicks** (beauty), **Lindex**, **Gina Tricot**, **Polarn O. Pyret** (children's clothes), **Lego**, **BRIO** (toys), **Design House Stockholm**, **Svenskt Tenn**, **Dalahäst** souvenirs, a pick-and-mix sweets shop.
  - **Services:** **Forex** (money exchange), a pharmacy (**Apoteket**, **Apotek Hjärtat**), **SJ** and **SL** ticket desks, the **Flygbussarna** desk, the car-rental desks.
  Each brand has its own numbers in `data/brands.js`: the rent it pays, the spend per passenger, whom it draws (families, business, tourists, children, transfers; Schengen or not), its opening hours, its size (kiosk, unit, big unit), the first year it can open and its sources. Kids' favourites lift the Passengers meter for families; a café near the gate lifts it for everyone in a delay.
- **Hotels:** **Radisson Blu Sky City**, **Clarion Hotel Arlanda Airport**, **Jumbo Stay**, in their real years (`data/brands.js` with sources).
- **Vehicles:** real makers where checked (Volvo and Scania trucks and buses; the snowplough and fire-truck makers actually used at Arlanda).
- **Rules for real brands:**
  - Every real company is shown **respectfully and truthfully**: its temper in the game comes from its public, documented way of doing business (a low-cost carrier asks for low fees and fast turnarounds; a long-haul A380 operator needs a Code F gate), never from invented misconduct.
  - **Random events never blame a real company:** a random breakdown is "the baggage system", a random cyber attack hits "the airport", a random late flight is weather or a turnaround step. Real historical events (a real bankruptcy, the ash cloud, the 2024 IT outage) are told as they happened, neutrally, in their real year.
  - **People are fictional** (managers, staff, the mentor), except public figures in historical facts (the King opening the airport in 1962).
  - The READMEs and the Archive's About card say: *a free, non-commercial fan game; not affiliated with or endorsed by Swedavia or any company shown; names and logos belong to their owners.*

## Look and style

- **View:** isometric 2:1 (like `city-builder`), procedural Canvas 2D drawings cached per zoom step, no photos. The whole airport fits on screen when zoomed out (runways as the dominant lines), and at full zoom one stand with its aircraft, the vehicles and the hi-vis people round it fills the screen; the airline's emblem on the fin is readable there.
- **Seasons and light are part of the identity:** a long dark winter (snow on everything but the cleared surfaces, blue afternoons, warm terminal windows, amber beacons, sodium-orange and later white LED apron lights, steam from engines), a bright short summer with **white nights** (around midsummer the sky never gets fully dark), autumn rain and fog, spring slush. The sun's height follows the real latitude (59.65° N) and date.
- **Palette tokens** (in `render/palette.js`, not in the logic): pine `#2E4A33`, spruce `#1F3326`, birch bark `#E8E4D8`, granite `#8A8F94`, lake `#3E6E8E`, Falu red `#8E2A1E`, snow `#F4F7FA`, asphalt `#3A3D41`, apron concrete `#B9B6AE`, taxiway line yellow `#F2C230`, terminal glass `#6F8FA6`, warm wood `#C89B63`, hi-vis yellow `#E8F23A`, hi-vis orange `#F28A1E`, beacon amber `#FFB000`. Brand colours come from the brand's own data, not from this palette. Interiors use light Scandinavian wood, white and grey stone, glass and daylight.
- **Aircraft** are real types of their era, drawn to scale (wingspan and length from `data/aircraft.js`, shared knowledge with `world-aviation`): DC-3, Convair 440, Caravelle, DC-8, DC-9, Boeing 727, 747, MD-80, Fokker 28 / 50 / 100, Saab 340 / 2000, BAe 146 / Avro RJ, Boeing 737 family, A320 family, ATR 72, CRJ, A330, 767, 777, 787, A350, A380, freighters, and in the future chapter an electric 30-seat commuter and a hydrogen prototype.
- **Swedish touches** next to the English: signs in Swedish and English ("Avgångar · Departures", "Utgång · Gate", "Bagage · Baggage"), fika in the staff room, *semester* in July, Lucia on 13 December, Midsummer, the snow crews' pride.

## Sources to check against

- Swedavia, *About Stockholm Arlanda Airport* and the airport's development plan (Terminal 5 expansion; the planned **Pier G** — 14 bridge-connected gates — was approved for detailed design in 2025 and **discontinued by Swedavia's board in November 2026**: in the future chapter the player decides on their own pier).
- Swedish AIP, ESSA AD 2 (aerodrome data and charts) — the runway and taxiway geometry.
- Wikipedia, *Stockholm Arlanda Airport* — history dates (official opening 1 April 1962 by King Gustav VI Adolf; first used for training flights in 1959), terminals, airlines and destinations, traffic by year.
- The airlines' and brands' own histories for their years at Arlanda, their liveries and logos by era.
- Photos of the terminals, piers, Sky City, the 2001 tower, Arlanda Express and Flygbussarna for the drawings.
