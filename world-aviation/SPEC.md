# World Aviation — SPEC

Developer/agent spec for the `world-aviation` project. Repo-wide conventions live in the root `SPEC.md` and `AGENTS.md`; this file only describes this game. Keep it in sync with the code.

## Idea

A career flight simulator in the browser: you are a young Swedish pilot with a fresh commercial licence (EASA CPL) flying out of **Stockholm Arlanda**, and earn the instrument rating, the ATPL and a captain's four stripes on the way. You are only the pilot — there is no company of your own: the clients are real airlines. The career starts with Swedish domestic flying, then Scandinavia and the North Atlantic, then — region by region — the whole world: Europe, the Middle East and Africa, the Americas, Asia and the Pacific. Every flight is a full cockpit-view departure-and-arrival between real airports — push back from the gate, start the engines, taxi, take off, handle whatever the weather and the aeroplane throw at you, land, and taxi to the gate. You earn money, reputation and licences, fly bigger aircraft (up to the Boeing 777, the Airbus A380, the Boeing 747-8F and the Antonov An-124), and take contracts from passenger airlines, freight companies and remote operators. At the very end waits the legend: the Antonov An-225 Mriya, the heaviest aeroplane ever flown, destroyed in 2022 — which the pilot builds again part by part (see The Mriya's assembly hall).

The game speaks **English, Russian or Swedish**, picked at the top of the title screen (see Language below); English is the source text. The sim is arcade-leaning but built on real forces (lift/drag/thrust, stall, weight, wind, icing, fuel burn, pressure altitude) rather than on fake "up = up" controls.

## Tech stack

HTML5 + CSS3 + vanilla ES2017 JavaScript, no build step, no package manager.

The 3D world is rendered with **three.js** (r147, the UMD build), **vendored into the project folder** as `lib/three.min.js` and loaded with a plain `<script src>` tag — no CDN, no bundler, no ES modules, so the folder still works offline, from `file://`, and when copied out of the repo on its own. It is a single vendored file, not a dependency the repo has to install. `WEBGL` is required for the flight view; if WebGL is unavailable the game says so and offers the rest of the career screens.

Rendering layout:

- **`lib/three.min.js`** (vendored, ~594 KB) — WebGL renderer, scene, camera, lighting.
- **WebGL canvas** — the world: sky dome shader, terrain mesh, sea, runways and taxiways (canvas-generated textures with real markings), buildings, trees, clouds, aircraft, other traffic.
- **Canvas 2D overlay** — the cockpit: window frame, glareshield, the full instrument panel (airspeed, attitude with the radio altitude, altimeter, HSI, VSI, engine gauges, warning lights), windshield effects (rain, snow, frost, fog, lightning) and the HUD; the rain and snow also fall in every outside view (moving with the camera: past the chase camera like past the cockpit, straight down past the tower). Rain and snow are particles in the air ahead of the cockpit (world metres around the eye, seen through the cockpit camera): they drift with the wind and fall, and the aeroplane flies through them, so parked the snow drifts down past the glass and at speed it streams out of the point the aeroplane is flying at; each particle is drawn as the streak it makes in a short exposure (`PRECIP_FX` in `ui/cockpit.js`). The particles move with the camera's own velocity, measured from its movement, so any view works.
- **The land cover** (`render/landcover.js`), painted over the terrain's own colours on both terrain meshes so the ground below shows a real landscape and its passing shows the speed from any height: a farmland tile (`QUALITY.landTex`: 512 texels on Low, 1024 on Medium/High, 5.2 km across) of field strips in blocks between lanes, tracks and hedges in the colours of the crops and the ploughed soil, farmsteads and nine villages (roofs and gardens along a grid of streets), made once as plain bytes and mipmapped; it repeats over the world with each copy turned, mirrored and shifted by its own dice, always by whole blocks, so the seams are lanes between fields (WebGL 2, `textureGrad` with the unbroken coordinate so the seams do not flicker; WebGL 1 repeats it plainly). The woods come from the tile's smooth noise read at 13.7 km, unbroken by the copies' seams, their edges following the fields a little, with the crowns and stands of trees in them; the regions — wooded or open country, the tone of the land — from the same noise at 33 and 47 km. How much shows comes from the terrain (`Terrain.colorAt`'s `land`): fields in the lowlands, none on the water, the beaches, rock, snow or sand (few in the deserts); woods thicker in the taiga, the tropics and the hills and from one region to the next; villages in open country, fewer in wooded land; the real cities of `data/cities.js` (`Terrain.urbanAt`, out to 1 + 4·√population km) grey blocks of roofs between the streets of a grid, their parks the woods. After dusk the villages' streets, the lone farms and the cities' grid of street lights glow sodium orange, read from a sharper mip level so far off they stay points. The 3D trees stand in its woods (`LandCover.forestAt`, the shader's sums on the CPU). The haze is about `VIEW.HAZE_M` (2 km) deep: above it the fog thins as the height grows (to `VIEW.HAZE_MIN` of its density), so from the cruise the ground below still shows.
- **Lights on the ground at night** (`render/lights3d.js`, built with the far terrain for each flight, faded in after dusk): the real towns and cities of `data/cities.js` — a bright white core, streets running out of it, sodium orange around, the size from the population; strings of road lights between neighbouring cities and from each airport to its city; villages over the land within 160 km of the route (fewer in the Arctic, the deserts and the mountains, each from its own cell's dice so they stay put). One `THREE.Points` object of fixed pixel size, `QUALITY.groundLights` points (14 000 / 32 000 / 60 000): half for the cities, a fifth for the roads, the rest for the villages. A 2D overlay keeps the gauges crisp and cheap and lets the cockpit frame be drawn on top of the 3D scene.
- **Web Audio API** — synthesized sounds, no audio files; spoken callouts with `speechSynthesis` where the browser has it.

Terrain: every flight builds a heightmap of its own part of the world (see below) and one near-field mesh that follows the aircraft (110–420 m cells depending on the quality preset and the height, heights and normals rebuilt on the CPU when the aircraft has moved far enough), so the ground stays detailed under the wheels without a huge vertex count. The far mesh sits 30 m under the real ground and the near mesh fades into it at its edge, so they never fight; that holds at its vertices, so every far vertex whose triangles can reach an airport's ground also goes under that field's elevation (`Terrain.farVertexHeight`) — otherwise a 4–6 km triangle between a low coastal field and the hills around it passes over the apron and hides it (Kalmar); the renderer uses a logarithmic depth buffer for the same reason.

**The world of a flight.** The globe cannot be one flat map, so every flight gets its own: an azimuthal equidistant projection centred on the great-circle midpoint of the route (`Theatre` in `utils.js`). The route is then a straight line of its true length, and nothing near it is distorted, anywhere on Earth (the date line included). The world is at its real size (`WORLD.SCALE` = 1; it used to be compressed to 0.45): the 3 400 nm Stockholm–New York leg is 3 400 nm of flying, about 7 hours at ×1 and some ten minutes on the autopilot at ×128. The near terrain mesh keeps its vertices relative to a local origin moved every 100 km, so it stays precise on the GPU thousands of kilometres from the middle of the world; the aircraft fuel tanks and ranges are honest for real distances, and the lease (per block hour) and the fuel price were scaled by 0.45 with the change so a flight earns what it did. `World.prepare(from, to)` (about a second, behind a "Preparing the route" screen) builds:

- **the terrain** (`terrain.js`): a grid over the route plus 500 km around it (at least 1 400 km across), 5–18 km real between samples (about 300 across). Land or sea comes from a scanline fill of the coastline polygons in `geodata.js` (every continent and the major islands, coarse — a degree or two — except Scandinavia, which is drawn in detail, and the Gulf of Antalya), with inland seas and lakes cut out (the Black Sea, the Caspian, the Great Lakes, Baikal, the Nordic lakes and fjords). The height comes from the distance to the coast, about 80 mountain ranges and plateaus (the Scandes, the Alps, the Himalaya and Tibet, the Rockies, the Andes, …) and fBm noise keyed on latitude and longitude, so a place looks the same on every flight (the value noise floors its coordinates: truncating them towards zero made it run far out of 0–1 west and south of 0°, with 8 800 m mountains beside Rio). The data being coarse, the ground round each airport is brought to its real elevation (`Terrain.fitAirports`): where the median of the ground 2–6 km round it is more than 150 m off the field, the land is moved by the excess, fully within 15 km and fading out by 60 km, so the relief near the field is kept relative to it and the mountains further off stay where the data puts them (Delhi stood on a Himalayan foothill, Mexico City in a pit 2 km deep, Bogotá on a table). The Taurus is two ranges round the Antalya plain (one straight ridge had buried Antalya's airport in a pit 1.5 km deep, the sea 60 km away). Colours follow the latitude in both hemispheres: tropical green, the great deserts, birch and pine in the north, tundra, and a snow line from 4 800 m in the tropics down to 560 m in the Arctic;
- **the airports** in that area, placed and laid out (`World.airports` / `World.here`); `World.list` / `World.byId` hold every airport's static data for the career and the screens.

Weather follows the latitude, the hemisphere (seasons are reversed in the south) and the field elevation.

World axes: x = east, y = up, z = south at the centre of the projection; headings are clockwise from the projection's north.

Airports are all built from one template around the runway in use (`LAYOUT` in `constants.js`): the runway is flown in its designator's direction for both take-off and landing (the surface wind is always within 70° of it), a parallel taxiway on the right with three exits and the holding point at the runway start, an apron lane with the gate stands (nose-in towards the terminal) and lanes off the taxiway into it (`World.apronLanes`: at both ends of the apron, in the gap between two terminals, and between a terminal's stands so that at most `LAYOUT.LANE_MAX_STANDS` (2) stands lie between two lanes — every stand has one beside it; a lane that would meet the taxiway within `LANE_SNAP_M` (50 m) of a runway exit meets it at the exit, a straight crossing, or moves 50 m from it, whichever stays `LANE_STAND_CLEAR_M` (25 m) from every stand — else, between the stands, it is left out), a terminal, a tower, hangars, a fuel farm and a cargo shed. Where two taxi lines meet at an angle (a corner, a T, a stand off the apron lane, a runway exit or the line-up off the runway) `World.buildFillets` puts an arc tangent to both (38 m radius on the taxiways, 30 m at the stands, 60 m off the runway on both sides of every exit, `LAYOUT.FILLET_R` / `FILLET_STAND_R` / `FILLET_EXIT_R`, less where a line is too short for it): the pavement gets a fillet there, the centreline and the edge lines curve round it — off the runway the yellow lead-off line leaves the runway centreline on the arc, as on a real airfield — and `Flight.surfaceAt` counts it as taxiway.

`airport3d.js` builds them in the airport's own frame (x across towards the terminal, z = −t down the runway):

- **The runway** has its own fine texture (up to 256 × 8192): asphalt with grain, repair patches and crack sealing, concrete ends with slab joints, rubber and tyre streaks in the touchdown zone, paved shoulders, 60 m blast pads with yellow chevrons, and the markings (threshold bar and piano keys, designators, touchdown zone bars, the aiming point at 400 m on long runways). Lights are `THREE.Points` of a fixed pixel size: white edge lights (yellow over the last 600 m), centreline lights (red/white, then red at the end), green threshold and red end bars, an approach light system to 900 m with a crossbar at 300 m and a sequenced flasher running towards the threshold, blue taxiway edge lights, green taxiway centreline lights every 30 m (round the curves too) — the taxiway lights are a set of their own (`rec.twyLights`), switched on only from dusk and in poor visibility (fading in from `dark` 0.08 to 0.4, and below 5 000 m of visibility, full below 1 500 m), off on a clear day. The lights draw without fog, so they show through haze much further than the ground (out to 2.5 times the visibility), and grow a little at night. The **PAPI** (four lights left of the runway, 300 m in) is live: each light is white when the eye is above its angle (3.5° / 3.17° / 2.83° / 2.5°) and red below, so on the glide path it shows two and two.
- **The ground texture** (4096 px along on Medium/High, 2048 on Low) has grass with mowing stripes and a lighter runway strip, taxiways and the runway connectors with sandy shoulders, the apron in concrete slabs with a service road, an oil stain on each stand, the landside road with a kerb, the car park full of cars, the shadows of the trees along the road, and the perimeter road inside the fence (the fence and the trees are geometry: see below). Its edges fade out into the terrain. Both planes are cut into cells of 60–100 m: the logarithmic depth buffer needs small triangles near the eye, or the terrain shows through (the buildings' walls are cut into cells of about 12 m for the same reason).
- **The fine markings** are thin strips of geometry just above the ground, not part of the ground texture (1–2 m a texel there, which smeared a half-metre line into a wide band), built in `Airport3D.buildMarkings`:
  - yellow, 0.16 m up: the taxi and apron centrelines (0.45 m, with a small disc at every joint so the turns have no gaps), the **taxiway edge lines** (0.45 m, just inside the pavement edge, round the outside of the whole network only: both sides and the round ends of every taxiway segment, with whatever falls on other pavement — another taxiway, the runway and its shoulders, the apron, a hangar pad — cut away, so the lines stop exactly where the taxiways meet and the outside of every bend is rounded), the curved centrelines round the fillets, the stand lead-in lines and stop bars, and the runway holding position (two solid and two dashed lines);
  - red, 0.155 m up: each stand's safety box and its dashed equipment restraint line;
  - white, 0.15 m up: the runway edge lines and centreline (0.9 m), the landside roads (solid edge lines, dashed lane lines, a solid line in the middle, round the corners on arcs of 45 m — the road itself is rounded (`roundPolyline`), so the asphalt, its lines and the cars follow the same curve — and ending where the ground texture fades into the terrain), the zebra crossings, and the lines of the apron service road and of the tail-of-stand road along the apron's airside edge. On desktops the renderer multisamples (antialias), so thin things — the markings, the terminal's window mullions — do not shimmer as you taxi past; phones keep the fill rate.
- **The look of each airport** (`AIRPORT_LOOK` in `data/airports.js`: a city symbol and a colour; the country's flag and greeting in `data/countries.js`): the airport's name in big letters on the terminal roof (facing the apron and the road), the roof slab and the tower band in the airport's colour, a "Welcome to <city>" banner with the city symbol, the local greeting (in Russia in Cyrillic, «Добро пожаловать»; elsewhere in Latin letters) and a flag strip on both fronts of the terminal, three flagpoles on the roof (country, city flag = the symbol on the airport colour, and the region's flag where there is one — else the country's again) and a flag over the tower. The region's flag (`AIRPORT_REGIONS` in `data/regions.js`, drawn by `art/regions.js`) flies at every Russian and Swedish airport: the Russian region's own flag (Moscow's St George, St Petersburg's anchors, Tatarstan, Sakha, Krasnoyarsk Krai, Primorye's tiger, Kamchatka's volcanoes, Chukotka, Udmurtia's black, white and red with the red sun sign at Izhevsk…), the banner of the Swedish province's arms (Uppland's orb at Arlanda and Bromma, Västergötland's lion at Landvetter, the Skåne flag at Malmö, Gotland's ram, Småland's lion, Blekinge's oak, Jämtland's elk, Medelpad's waves, Västerbotten's reindeer, Norrbotten's quartered arms) and the Sámi flag at Kiruna. At the airports of a country whose language the game is in (Russian at a Russian airport, Swedish at a Swedish one) the name on the roof is in that language (`roofLabel` in `render/airport3d.js`: `aptName(a, lang)` — «ИЖЕВСК», «САНКТ-ПЕТЕРБУРГ — ПУЛКОВО», «GÖTEBORG LANDVETTER»), and the welcome banner with it: the local greeting on top, the city in that language, "Welcome to <city> · <country>" under it; an airport built in another language is built again when the next flight comes to it (`Scene3D.buildAirport` compares the label). The flags and the windsock turn downwind and stream or droop with the surface wind (`Flight.surfaceWindNow()`: the airfield's wind and its gusts, never the wind where the aeroplane is, so they do not swing as it turns), followed slowly (about 3 s) so the gusts do not make them twitch; a flag is bent vertex by vertex about its hoist edge, which stays on the pole (the cloth never swings through it), and a slow, shallow wave runs along it on its own clock (a change of the wind speeds the wave up gently, never jumps it). The cloth's bounding sphere holds every shape the wind can give it (or a drooping flag was culled as a streaming one and blinked at the edges of the view while turning), and its texture is a power of two, mipmapped everywhere. The tower and the hangars grow with the airport (`TOWER_H`, `HANGARS` in `constants.js`): a tiny airport has a 10 m square concrete post beside a low operations building with a small flared cab, a small one a slender 20 m round shaft with a balcony in the airport's colour and an eight-sided cab, a medium one the 32 m round tower with a wide cab, a big one a 56 m tapering shaft on a base building, two rings in the airport's colour, an equipment floor, a big cab and a radar dome and an aerial on the roof. The hangars stand side by side down the runway from the apron, their doors on one line (`LAYOUT.HANGAR_DOORS`), in three shapes: a small shed with a pitched roof (`gable`), the arched hangar (`arch`: a half cylinder across, its end caps above the walls, never in their plane, which flickered; the pitched roof is the same with two sides) and a big maintenance hangar for the wide-bodies (`wide`: a flat roof with a raised middle over the tail dock, a deep door frame in the airport's colour over six door leaves) — one shed at a tiny airport, an arched hangar and a shed at a small one, two arched and a shed at a medium one, and a maintenance hangar, two arched and a shed at a big one. Each airport paints its buildings in one of eight schemes picked by its code (`BUILDING_PAINTS` in `render/airport3d.js`: concrete grey, sand, white and blue, brick, dark steel, cream and green, falu red sheds, silver with red roofs) — the terminal's walls, the tower, the hangars and the cargo shed, their roofs and doors; about thirty big, well-known airports have something like their real colours instead (`REAL_PAINTS`, over the concrete grey: e.g. Pulkovo's gilded roof, Denver's white tent roof, Jakarta's terracotta roofs, Gardermoen's oak, the raw concrete of Charles de Gaulle, Brandenburg's limestone, and the hangars in the home carrier's colours — KLM, Lufthansa, Aeroflot, Emirates, Qatar, Singapore, Cathay), which may also paint the terminal's roof slab (`termRoof`, else the airport's colour). The hangars carry the home airlines' logos, the cargo shed a cargo carrier's, and the parked aeroplanes at the gates wear the home airlines' liveries (`airlinesAt` in `data/airlines.js`). Also a localiser array past the far end, a glideslope mast beside the touchdown zone on the side away from the taxiway (beside it, 17 m off the taxiway's centreline, it stood in the way of the wings), a red runway holding sign and yellow exit signs. The taxiways are ICAO Annex 14's width for the biggest aeroplane the airport takes (`LAYOUT.TWY_WIDTHS`): 25 m at a big airport (code F, the A380), 23 m at a medium one (E), 18 m at a small one (D), 15 m at the smallest (C). The floodlight masts on the airside of the apron stand on the grass just off it, 52 m from the apron lane's centreline and from every lane off the taxiway (`MAST_CLEAR`, ICAO code F: 50.5 m from a taxilane to an object), clear of every wing and of the service road: they stand only between two lanes at least twice that apart, spread evenly about 110 m from each other (`a.apronLanes`). Each big airport has its own sign style for its terminals (`signStyle`: one of eight typefaces and a weight, and an accent colour from the city's, shifted a little — there is no international standard for the look of terminal signs): the terminals without the airport's name on the roof carry a panel with their number in a square and TERMINAL, and the TERMINAL n signs on the apron and the road side are in the same style.
- **Life on the apron** (`render/apron3d.js`): a sharp stand number plate on each lead-in line and a stand board on the terminal (standing out from the glass on a dark box, clear of the window frames); at every stand with a parked aeroplane a jet bridge to its front door (medium and big terminals; at small ones an airstair truck): the door is where the livery paints it (`frontDoorFromNose`), the bridge's cab turned square to the fuselage whatever the tunnel's angle, the bellows against its side where the body is widest under them (`fuselageRing`: by the nose it narrows), so the bridge never cuts into the aeroplane, a GPU, a belt loader at the aft hold, a fuel truck under a jet's wing (at least 13.5 m out from the fuselage, clear of the baggage trains), a catering truck at a big jet, cones at the nose, the wingtips and the tail. The stands the player uses this flight are empty, their bridge retracted: folded back along the terminal between the glass and the service road, clear of both. Floodlight masts along the apron's airside edge (26 m) and, on the terminal side, halfway between the stands (the first one 30 m before the first stand, clear of the welcome banner), 7.5 m out from the glass — outside the roof's overhang, the lamp head turned along the building, so no mast goes through the roof — and 2.5 m lower than the roof edge, so they never stand in front of the airport's name on the roof; the airside row leaves a gap in front of the name (its length along the roof and `MAST_NAME_GAP` 40 m either side, `roofName` in `render/airport3d.js`), where a 26 m mast would cross the letters seen from the apron and the taxiway, and spreads its masts over what is left; traffic: the baggage trains, cars on the landside roads. **The baggage trains** (`render/baggage3d.js`) work like the real ones and never come near the taxiway or the apron lane: every terminal has a baggage hall with a door in its front at the building's end (a dark opening in a yellow frame, the follow-me car parked beside it), and one to three trains (a tractor with an amber beacon and three carts; about 0.7 per served stand) that come out of it, drive along the service road between the stands and the building keeping right (3.5 m either side of its middle), turn off between two stands down past the belt loader (22 m out from the fuselage's side), make a U-turn and come up to stop beside it (10 m out), facing out. A train takes the bags out to a departure and comes back empty, or goes out empty to an aeroplane that has come in and brings its bags back (each trip one or the other at random); while it waits at the stand (22–40 s) the bags go on or off the carts one by one, then it drives back into the hall and comes out again 6–22 s later for another stand of its terminal, one train per stand. The player's own aeroplane at rest at its stand is served too: the bags out before a departure, in after an arrival. Every corner is rounded (7 m) and the carts follow the tractor's track, so a train bends through a turn; it drives at 6.5 m/s, slows to 2.6 m/s for the turns, pulls away and stops smoothly, and waits for another train just ahead of it (at most 8 s, so two never lock each other). The trains give way to the player's aeroplane while it moves on the ground (`Apron3D.blocked`): the ground the aeroplane covers is its circle (half its length or span + 8 m, `GIVE_WAY_PAD`) where it is now and where its speed takes it in the next 8 s (`GIVE_WAY_AHEAD_S`); a train whose track meets that ground within 30 m ahead (`GIVE_WAY_GAP`) stops there, well short of it, and waits; one already on that ground drives on and clears it, so none is left standing across the aeroplane's way. Static parts are merged into one vertex-coloured mesh per group (`kit()`), so a busy apron is a handful of draw calls. The same goes for the buildings, the landside and the boards (`Merge3D.bake` in `Airport3D.build`): baked into a mesh per material, materials that look the same counted as one, the ones lit at night apart (with those lit in the same colour), the flags' cloth left out as it moves in the wind. A big airport seen whole (on the final, on the apron) was about 1 300 draw calls, more than half of them the parked aeroplanes; baked, about 400. During the push back a tug sits at the nose gear.
- **The terminal's signs and the landside** (`render/landside3d.js`): the terminals and their stands grow with the airport (`LAYOUT.TERMINAL_PLANS`, by its size and runway): a tiny airport one terminal with one stand, a small one two stands, a medium one three (four from a 3 000 m runway), a big one two terminals of three stands, three of three from 3 300 m, three of four from 3 600 m and three of five from 3 900 m — from 1 × 1 to 3 × 5; the terminals of a big airport stand 70 m apart (`LAYOUT.TERMINAL_GAP`): TERMINAL n over the doors on the road side, a Tn · TERMINAL n sign under the roof on the apron side between its stands, the airport's name on the roof of the middle one and TERMINAL n on the others, the welcome banner on T1 and the flags on the last; in front of each a zebra crossing and its number painted on the road (Airport3D.roadNumbers). Medium and small airports have one terminal with DEPARTURES and ARRIVALS over its doors. A stand is named with its terminal first where there are several ("Terminal 2, gate 3", "T2 · gate 3"), as the signs go from the terminal to the gate. Behind the car park stand more buildings the bigger the airport is (`LANDSIDE` in `constants.js`, `a.landside` from `World.buildBuildings`) on a paved forecourt: a small airport an office block; a medium one a multi-storey car park, a hotel and an office block; a big one a bigger car park, a hotel and two office towers. The blocks have a band of windows on every floor, all round, and plant on the roof; the hotel has HOTEL on its roof facing the apron and the road; the car park has open decks with parapets, columns and cars, stair towers and blue P signs. A tiny airport has none.
- **The local products' boards** (`render/adverts3d.js`, at the Russian and Swedish airports): the products of the region and of the country (`AIRPORT_REGIONS`, `NATIONAL_BRANDS` and `BRANDS` in `data/regions.js`; the boards painted by `art/brands.js` — the brand's name, a small line, its colours and a simple mark, not the real logos): Ижмолоко and ИЖ at Izhevsk, Алёнка at Sheremetyevo, Север and Петмол at Pulkovo, Чак-чак and КАМАЗ at Kazan, АЛРОСА at Yakutsk, Норникель at Norilsk; Volvo and Göteborgs Kex at Landvetter, Skånemejerier and Pågen at Malmö, IKEA at Växjö, Norrmejerier and Västerbottensost at Umeå, LKAB and Icehotel at Kiruna; then the country's — СБЕР, Простоквашино, Почта России, Пятёрочка, РЖД; Arla, Marabou, ICA, Kalles, PostNord, SJ. They stand: on the terminal's apron front high under the roof, 22 m from each stand on the side of its bridge's rotunda (above the bridge; `ADVERT_FROM_STAND`, at most 6 m tall, 3:1), wherever it is clear of the welcome banner and the Tn · TERMINAL n sign (`b.frontTaken`), seen from the cockpit at the gate; on two double-sided billboards on legs beside the access road (`BILLBOARD`); and in big letters on the office roofs behind the car park, facing the apron and the road (the airport's own: СБЕР, VOLVO, LKAB, IKEA…). They are lit at night. The names are scenery and never translated.
- **The big terminals** (`terminalStyle`, `Airport3D.terminalRoof`): a big airport's terminal is 26 m tall (22 before; medium 16, small 11) and wears a roof of its own over the box, in one of four designs — the real one's look where it is known (`TERMINAL_STYLES` in `render/airport3d.js`), else one by the airport's code: **wing** — one sweeping roof over the whole building, a low lip 15 m out over the apron rising to a crest near the road side (about 15 m over the box), waving gently along it, on raked struts in the airport's colour between the stands (Madrid, Beijing, Doha, Heathrow, Istanbul, Baku); **vaults** — a row of 34 m barrel vaults across the building, glass in their ends framed by a rib in the airport's colour (Kuala Lumpur, Paris, Frankfurt, Sheremetyevo, Tashkent); **tents** — two staggered rows of white membrane peaks on masts (Denver, Johannesburg, Miami); **folded** — a sawtooth of folded plates across the building, the ridges rising to 16 m over the box at tall glazed gables over the apron (Pulkovo's gilded, Oslo, Helsinki, Arlanda, Schiphol). The shell is in the roof colour (`termRoof`, else a light metal), its edge in the airport's colour, the glass under it lit at night; the glass front runs 80 % of the height. Each design sets `b.roofAt(t, across)` and `b.roofTop(across)`, so the name on the roof, the TERMINAL n panels and the flagpoles stand on it (the letters on legs down to the box where the roof under them dips).

## Time of day

The briefing offers four departure times (`TIME_OF_DAY`, remembered in the career, `Career.timeOfDay`): **Day** 13:00, **Dusk** 17:42 (+5 % of the pay), **Night** 23:00 (+15 %), **Dawn** 06:18 (+5 %); the bonus is a line on the invoice ("Night flight"). The clock starts at the departure's local time and runs with the flight (`env.hour0` + the flight time), shown on the strip as LOCAL TIME at the departure (and at the same moment at the arrival), so a long dusk flight lands in the dark. The sun follows a generic path (`sunAt` in `utils.js`: equinox, latitude 50°: up at 6, 40° high at noon, down at 18).

- **The sky**: day, dusk and night colours mixed by the sun's height; at dusk the horizon glows on the sun's side; the sky is seen from where the aeroplane is (`Theatre.toGeo` of its position): its latitude, and its local solar time — the departure's clock plus 1 h for every 15° of longitude flown east (minus, west), so flying east the sky turns faster, flying west slower and a westbound evening flight follows the sunset; north is the world's north (−z, as the runways and the compass have it). At night 2 800 stars on a whole sphere turning round the pole (as high in the north as the latitude, below the horizon south of the equator; a bright pole star on it) at the sidereal rate (`SIDEREAL_RATE`) with the clock, the month (`Career.data.season`) setting which are out at night: they rise in the east and set in the west, fade towards the horizon (none below it), fade in as it darkens and in poor visibility, and their turning shows plainly with time sped up. The moon has a phase per flight (`env.moonPhase`, 0 new, 0.5 full, drawn from the contract's id, so a retry of the flight has the same moon), ageing with the flight time (`MOON_MONTH_DAYS`); it keeps 24 h × phase behind the sun on the same path (full: opposite the sun, rising at dusk; a quarter: highest at dusk or dawn; new: with the sun, unseen), drawn as a ball lit from the sun's real direction (crescent, half, gibbous, full) with a few grey seas and a faint earthshine on a crescent's dark side, pale by day. Its light at night (pale blue, from its direction) grows with its lit share and its height; with the moon down the night is darker, lit from the sky's glow. The fog, the clouds and the ambient light take the colour of the hour.
- **The aircraft lights** (`AircraftModels.build`: points of a fixed pixel size, bigger in the dark): red and green navigation lights on the wingtips and a white one on the tail, a red beacon on top and under the belly (flashing once a second), white strobes on the wingtips (a double flash every 1.2 s), landing lights in the wing roots and a taxi light on the nose gear. The crew's logic (`Scene3D.lightsFor`): navigation lights and beacon with the engines running, strobes on the runway and in the air, landing lights with the gear down on the runway and below 3 000 ft, the taxi light when taxiing. Parked aeroplanes are dark.
- **The landing light pool**: in the dark a soft pool of light lies where the landing (or taxi) lights' beam meets the ground ahead, seen from the cockpit too: on a night approach the runway comes up in it.

## Practice landing

From the briefing, **Practice the landing · {fee}** starts the contract's flight already on the final at the destination: on the glide path, as far out as it flies in 10 s (`PRACTICE.AP_SECONDS`) so that the autopilot hands over 3 nm out (`PRACTICE.HANDOVER_NM`; the start is 3 nm + the speed × 10 s), **clean** (gear and flaps up, about Vref + 25: the configuration is part of the practice; the prompt asks for the gear, the flaps and Vref), at the chosen time of day, the arrival weather, no emergencies, the strip headed PRACTICE LANDING. The autopilot flies the ILS for 10 s of flight (`PRACTICE.AP_SECONDS`, counted in simulated seconds), then disconnects with "Your controls — gear, flaps, land and brake below 35 kt"; you land and brake below `SIM.ROLLOUT_EXIT_KT` on the runway, and the practice is over.

- **The fee** is paid when it starts: 5 % of the aircraft's hourly lease, at least 100 kr (`PRACTICE.FEE_LEASE_SHARE`, `FEE_MIN`; 100 kr in the Beechcraft 1900D, 500 kr in the 737). Restarting from the pause is a new session and is paid again.
- **A landing** is graded like a real one (`Game.gradeLanding`) and earns reputation with the contract's client group: A+ 0.5, A 0.4, B 0.2, C 0.1 (`PRACTICE.REP`). Only the improvement on that contract's best practice counts (`contract.practiceRep`), so repeating a landing does not farm reputation. Cheats void it.
- **No landing** — a crash, a runway excursion, flying past the runway, a touch-and-go, climbing above 3 000 ft AGL or flying 2 nm further out: no damage bill, no lost reputation, no crash in the statistics; only the fee is gone.
- **The result screen** shows the grade and the touchdown numbers (or what went wrong), the reputation gained, the fee, and three buttons: **Try again** (a new paid session), **Back to the briefing**, **Fly it for real**.

## Flight model

Fixed 60 Hz physics step with render interpolation, world in metres (x = east, z = north, y = altitude MSL), speeds in knots internally.

Forces, all real:

- **Lift** `L = ½·ρ·V²·S·CL(α, flaps, slats, ice)` — `CL` from a linear curve with a soft stall break past the critical α, plus stall behaviour: the nose drops, lift collapses, a stall warning sounds at `α` slightly before the break.
- **Drag** `CD = CD0 + k·CL²` with `CD0` raised by gear, flaps, ice and (in a go-around) the spoiler.
- **Thrust** per engine, scaled by throttle, density ratio `σ` (so climb performance decays with altitude) and by airspeed (propellers lose static thrust with speed).
- **Weight** affects `CL` needed, stall speed (`Vs = √(2mg/(ρ S CLmax))`), climb rate, acceleration and touchdown distance — a heavy jet flies nothing like an empty bush plane.
- **Wind** — surface wind per airport (direction/speed), gradient wind increasing with altitude, gusts, turbulence noise, and local effects (sea breeze, rotor near high ground, crosswind on landing).
- **Ground** — normal reaction, tyre friction, nosewheel steering with rudder (hydraulic: the nosewheel turns at most 22°/s, `SIM.NOSEWHEEL_STEER_RATE_DEG`, and the turn rate builds up at most 0.9 rad/s², `SIM.GROUND_YAW_ACCEL`, so a turn starts gently), differential braking, weight-on-wheels, runway/taxiway surface detection, wet/icy grip.
- **Control surfaces** — moved by hydraulic actuators: the ailerons, the elevator and the rudder follow the stick or the keys at 2.0 / 1.6 / 1.2 full deflections a second (`CONTROLS.SURFACE_RATE`), so a key press does not throw a surface to its stop at once (the autopilot sets them directly).
- **Systems** — flaps 0–5 with limits (`Vfe`/`Vlo`: never a lock — the levers always move, out too fast the flap or gear overspeed warning sounds and the flaps take damage), gear with transit time and doors, spoiler, anti-skid, stall break, overshoot at touchdown (`sink rate > 600 fpm` = damage, `> 1000 fpm` = gear collapse).

Ground operations are a real part of the game: push back, engine start (starter + fuel boost + N2 spool-up, watch the gauge), taxi with the guidance arrow, stop at the hold line, line up, takeoff, then after landing roll out, exit at the first taxiway, taxi along the taxiway centreline with a deviation indicator, and stop inside the **parking box** at the assigned gate, aligned, parking brake set, engines shut down.

**The guidance arrow** (`sim/guidance.js`, drawn by `ui/hud.js`) points at a carrot a little ahead on the taxi route (pure pursuit, `max(30 m, 0.65 × the length)`), so it already turns to the next leg as you come up to a corner. On the ground it is drawn lying on the ground, as the camera sees it (`HUD.groundArrow`): turned by the way to the carrot against where the camera looks along the ground — not the nose, so from the front, the wing or the tower it points the way from that side — and tilted back (CSS `perspective` + `rotateX`) by how flat the camera looks at the ground: flat from straight above (the top-down views), laid back looking along the ground, at most `VIEW.TAXI_ARROW_MAX_TILT_DEG` (58°) so it never turns edge-on; in the air it stays turned in the screen's plane, up the screen the nose. It never points back at a turn already passed:
- the leg you are on is followed in order (`World.routeProgress` keeps it on the route, `route.seg`): it moves on to the next leg once that one is nearer and this one is mostly behind you, back only when the previous one is much nearer — a later leg passing close by (the apron lane beside the taxiway) never takes over;
- a missed turn — more than `SIM.REROUTE_DEVIATION_M` (25 m) off the route with its nearest point behind you — gives a new route from the nearest junction ahead (off the runway, no other stand);
- on the landing roll the exit to take is the first one still reachable at the present speed: the room to slow to the taxi speed at `SIM.EXIT_DECEL_MS2` plus `EXIT_TURN_M` for the turn, at least `EXIT_MIN_LEAD_M` ahead. Roll past it and the arrow moves on to the next exit (the runway's end at the last); once you turn off (`EXIT_COMMIT_DEG` off the runway heading, or off the runway) the exit you took stays. While the exit is still more than a carrot ahead, the arrow points at the runway's edge where the exit's turn-off leaves it (`SIM.EXIT_AIM_BACK_M` before the exit): almost straight ahead while it is far, leaning more and more towards it as it comes up — never at the runway's end unless that is the exit;
- lining up, it leads onto the centreline ahead of you, never back to the line-up point.

**Taxi speed** (`sim/taxilimit.js`, `TAXI` in `constants.js`). The limits are by the **ground speed** — the airspeed is no use on the ground, the wind moves it (into a 15 kt headwind the airspeed reads 30 kt at 15 kt over the ground): **30 kt** on a straight taxiway (`STRAIGHT_KT`), **15 kt** through a turn of the taxi route (`TURN_KT`: a corner turning more than `TURN_MIN_DEG` 30°, over its curve — the fillet's radius there, `World.buildFillets` — and `TURN_PAD_M` 10 m either side; the turn off the runway at the exit too) and **10 kt** on the apron lane and into the stand (`APRON_KT`); off a route (starting the engines on the apron lane, at the holding point) the apron's or the taxiway's by where the aeroplane is. Before a slower stretch the limit comes down along a gentle braking (`DECEL_MS2` 0.8 m/s²), so it never drops by a step under the wheels and there is always room to slow down for it. Each frame `fl.taxi` holds the ground speed, the limit here, the limit with what is coming (`now`), the limit to show (the one here, or the slower one ahead once it brings `now` down) and a level: 0 within `now`, 1 over it, 2 over 1.5 times it (`RED_OVER`). It is shown in two places: under the taxi arrow, a small dark tag with the ground speed in big figures — green, **amber** over the limit, **red** over 1.5 times it — and under it the stretch and its limit ("Taxiway ≤ 30 kt", "Turn ahead ≤ 15 kt", "Apron ≤ 10 kt"; it stays when the arrow is gone, at the holding point); and on the panel the airspeed gauge turns into **GND SPEED** while taxiing: the needle and the figures on the ground speed, the figures in the same colours, the limit as a gold bug (the V-speed bugs and the TAS gone). The prompt says "slow down to 15 kt" (amber) or "too fast — slow down to 15 kt" (red). A **taxi overspeed** is twice the limit **here** (`FINE_OVER`), off the runway — the warning, a fine of 3 % and the reputation as before.

**The first officer taxis in** (`sim/copilot.js`, `COPILOT`). Once the pilot has flown **5 flights in the type** (`COPILOT.FLIGHTS`, `Career.flightsIn`: the hangar card's ✈ Flights), **Enter** (touch: **Go**) after the landing hands the taxi to the stand to the first officer ("First officer: my controls — taxiing to Gate 3"): the time goes to **×4** (`COPILOT.TIME_ACCEL`, the only time acceleration on the ground; **R** / **T** between ×1 and ×4) and the first officer works the throttle, the brakes and the nosewheel. It steers for the arrow's point (pure pursuit: the curve through it with the steering the speed allows; still rolling down the runway, the centreline a carrot ahead until the exit's curve begins — the guidance then keeps the exit picked when it took over, as long as it is ahead), keeps 85 % of the limit (`SPEED_SHARE`), slowing down for a slower stretch earlier than the limit asks (`DECEL_MS2` 0.55 m/s²), on a throttle with an integral (a heavy aeroplane needs more to get rolling) and brakes from 0.3 m/s too fast (idle thrust alone rolls a light type on). **40 m before the stand's lead-in leaves the apron lane** (`HANDOVER_M`) it stops and gives the controls back ("your controls — turn in to Gate 3 and stop on the stop bar"), the time back at ×1: parking is the pilot's. Enter again, the stick or the brakes past half (`TAKEOVER_INPUT`), the throttle keys or slider, a throttle preset or the parking brake take the controls back at once ("You have control"); Enter hands them over again while there is still room before the handover point ("Nearly there — park it yourself" when not). Before the 5 flights the prompt says how many are flown ("after 5 flights in this type the first officer taxis for you (2 of 5)") and Enter says what it waits for. Checked headless (75 runs: five airports from one stand to fifteen, five types from the Twin Otter to the A380, calm and an 18 kt crosswind): every run stops at the handover point, never red, never fined, in 1¼–4 minutes of game time — 20 s to a minute of real time.

**The FOLLOW ME car** (`render/followme3d.js`, `FOLLOW_ME`). On the pilot's first landing at an airport (`Career.visitsTo` the arrival is 0; `fl.followMe`) the tower says a FOLLOW ME car waits past the exit and leads to the stand, and the taxi prompt says "follow the FOLLOW ME car". A yellow car with black and yellow checks along its sides, a lit black board on its roof with FOLLOW ME in yellow facing back at the pilot, and an amber beacon flashing (brighter at night). It waits on the taxiway `WAIT_M` (25 m) past the exit the arrow picks (moving on to the next one while the landing roll passes an exit); once the aeroplane comes up it drives ahead of it along the taxi route — `GAP_M` (30 m) plus `LEAD_S` seconds of the aeroplane's speed past the nose, waiting when it stops, never backing up, never faster than `MAX_KT` (45 kt) — and by the stand drives on along the apron lane `PARK_M` (70 m, less where the apron ends) and stops there, out of the way, until the flight is over. After a missed turn it carries on from where it is on the new route. It leads the first officer just the same.

**Collisions** (`sim/collide.js`, `sim/airframe.js`, `COLLIDE` in `constants.js`). The buildings and the parked aeroplanes are solid. Every physics step the own aeroplane — spheres along its fuselage, its wings, its tailplane and fin and its engines (`Airframe.samples`, from the same layout the 3D model is built by, `modelLayout`), turned by its heading, pitch and bank — is tested against the nearest airport's obstacles: its buildings as boxes (the terminals, the tower with its cab, `TOWER_CAB_M` over `TOWER_H`, the hangars, the fuel farm, the cargo warehouse, the landside behind the terminal) and the aeroplanes parked at its stands as upright prisms of their type (the fuselage, each wing, the tailplane, the fin, the engines: `Airframe.solids`). The type at each stand is `gate.parked` (`World.parkedType`, `PARKED_TYPES` by the terminal's size), drawn by `render/airport3d.js`. The stands the flight uses have no parked aeroplane (`Flight.gatesInUse`, `World.standsFor`, the same list hides them in the scene); an aeroplane wider than the airport's stands are made for (`LAYOUT.MAX_SPAN`: the An-225 anywhere, the An-124 or the A380 at a medium airport) gets the stands on either side of its own kept free too, at both ends. What happens:
- in the air, or on the ground at `CRASH_KT` (6 kt) or more: the flight is lost — "Collision — you hit the terminal at 14 kt." / "Collision — you flew into the control tower.";
- slower, on the ground: a touch — the aeroplane stops dead where it was last clear (so it can turn away), "You touched a parked Boeing 737-800 at 3 kt — the aircraft is damaged", the damage `BUMP_DAMAGE` (0.3) at 6 kt down to a quarter of it at a crawl, once per obstacle until it has been clear of it for `BUMP_REPEAT_S`; the repairs are on the debrief.
Not tested while the tug has the aeroplane (`GATE`, `PUSHBACK`) or once it is parked (`SHUTDOWN`, `PARKED`). The stand (`LAYOUT.STAND`, 380 m across from the centreline, the apron lane 95 m before it) leaves the noses of the types up to the A330 and the 787 short of the apron service road in front of the glass (`LAYOUT.SERVICE_ROAD`, 420 m), the An-225's 18 m short of the terminal on the stop bar, a 747-8's 22 m; the parked aeroplanes' tails clear the apron lane for every type. `tools/collide-check.js` drives every type along every taxi route of every airport and parks it on every stand (below the file tree).

## Landing and scoring

ILS-style guidance on the approach: localiser and glideslope (3°) deviation diamonds and an "on glide path" callout; a flight path marker shows where the aircraft is going. Scored on:

- touchdown vertical speed (fpm),
- distance from the threshold (touchdown zone),
- centreline offset at touchdown,
- crab/de-crab angle at touchdown,
- speed vs. `Vref`,
- rollout: stopped before the end, or exited at the first taxiway,
- taxi-in: time, centreline deviations, final stop in the parking box,
- damage and fuel used vs. plan.

Overall grade A+ … F feeds the payout multiplier (0.4 … 1.35).

## Autopilot and time acceleration

- **Y** engages the autopilot in the air (moving the stick hard disconnects it). Laterally it flies **NAV** (the default, **N**): to an initial approach fix 15 nm before the threshold on the extended centreline, then it captures the localiser and tracks it as a track (crabbing into the wind). The turn onto it is anticipated (`navTarget`, `locIntercept`): at every distance from the centreline there is a steepest closing angle from which a turn still rolls out on the line — a circle of the autopilot's radius at 25° of bank (`SIM.AP_BANK_DEG`) × 1.25 (`LOC_TURN_MARGIN`) plus 3 s of rolling in (longer for a type that rolls slowly, `AP_ROLL_IN_S` × 0.85 / its roll rate), worked out at the faster of the ground speed now and the ground speed it will have on the final with the wind along the runway; close to the line no steeper than closing the gap in 22 s (`LOC_TIME_S`). Near the approach (where the turn ends at least 5 nm out, `LOC_MIN_EST_NM`) the localiser is captured the moment the closing angle reaches that limit, then the commanded track follows the limit down to the line (never more than straight at it, except when turning in from outbound), with the bank of that circle fed forward so the autopilot does not lag behind the curve: even a jet turning in at 90° or from behind stays within a few tens of metres of the centreline (a heavy in a strong tailwind within ~250 m). From the runway side, heading out, it first flies to an entry point beside the fix (1.9 turn radii to the side), where the turn back onto the final fits. Below its selected altitude it never descends under the **minimum safe altitude**: the highest ground 20 km ahead (and 1.5 km to each side) + 300 m (1 000 ft, a real minimum safe altitude's obstacle clearance, `MSA_MARGIN_M`), until it is established in the approach corridor. It climbs at the type's `climbRate` low down and less and less higher up, as a real aeroplane's engines have less to spare (`Flight.apClimbRate`): the rate falls in a straight line to nothing at 1.5 × the type's cruise level (`AP_CEILING_RATIO`), never below a quarter of it (`AP_CLIMB_MIN_SHARE`) — an ATR 72 climbs at 1 280 fpm off the ground and 500 fpm at 20 000 ft and takes 24 min to get there (a long leg cruises at 92 % of the type's cruise level: 20 000 ft for the ATR, whose real ceiling is 25 000 ft), a 737 takes 22 min to 34 000 ft. A climb — for the terrain, or back up to the selected altitude — is only as steep as the speed over 1.3 × the stall speed allows, and the speed brake stays in while the terrain holds the aeroplane up (high above the profile then, it climbed with the speed brake out, slowed, stalled and dropped the autopilot). **; '** switch to **HDG** mode and turn the selected heading. Vertically it holds the selected altitude (**, .**, 500 ft steps; set automatically for the climb and the descent). Engaging it takes the altitude the flight plan wants now (the cruise level, or 2 500 ft over the arrival after the top of descent) — not the one the aeroplane is at, so a pilot who has not climbed high enough is taken up — unless the pilot chose one with **, .** or a checklist set one (`ap.altSet`; **N** goes back to the programme). A checklist's altitude (`setAlt` / `setAltBy`: icing 2 000 ft lower, out of the cloud — in the climb 2 000 ft under the level it climbs to, never back down to the ground; the cabin altitude 10 000 ft) holds only while the emergency lasts (`ap.emergency`, `Game.releaseEmergencyAlt`): the ice gone with the anti-ice on, or the cabin holding its pressure again, and `SIM.EMERGENCY_ALT_HOLD_S` (2 min of flight) later, the autopilot climbs back to the flight plan's altitude ("The emergency is over — autopilot back to the flight plan"); until then the cruise prompt says the altitude is held for the emergency and that **N** takes it back now. Held for good, as it was, a whole leg was flown at 2 300 ft at the arrival's floor — a 737 at 250 kt true instead of 430, Beijing–Yakutsk more than two hours behind the schedule; on the approach the glideslope takes over and, on the localiser, captures the glideslope from below (or from at most 0.35° above it, `GS_CAPTURE_ABOVE_DEG`) and descends on it. It disconnects at 200 ft AGL (decision height) — the landing is always flown by hand. An autothrottle holds the cruise speed and slows down by a schedule (`Flight.apSpeedTarget`): 250 kt below 10 000 ft over the arrival (`AP_SLOW_KT`), Vref + 50 kt at 20 nm (coming down 6 kt per nm from further out), Vref + 30 on the approach, Vref + 5 in the last 5 nm — never below Vref (on the approach) or 1.35 × the stall speed, never above the flap and gear limits. The descent is flown like a real autopilot's: more than 100 m above the selected altitude (`AP_IDLE_DESCENT_M`) the thrust is at idle and the speed is on the elevator — faster than the schedule, the descent gets shallower (0.6 m/s per kt, down to level flight, `AP_DESCENT_SPEED_GAIN`) until the drag has taken the speed off. The descent profile is 3.2 nm per 1 000 ft down to 2 500 ft over the arrival 12 nm out (`DESCENT_NM_PER_KFT`, `DESCENT_END_NM`; the top of descent is planned on it); more than 500 ft above it and still fast, the autopilot puts the speed brake out itself and takes it in again on the profile ("Autopilot: speed brake out — high on the descent"; a pilot who works the lever takes it over, and it goes in when the autopilot is switched off). So a jet arrives on the localiser at about Vref + 50 and can take its flaps, instead of reaching the runway at 250 kt. On the approach the prompt asks for the flaps from 15 nm, a step whenever the speed allows it (`FLAPS_PROMPT_NM`), and for the gear from 8 nm (`GEAR_PROMPT_NM`), so the aeroplane is configured on the glideslope. It moves the thrust levers smoothly: the speed error drives them, the smoothed speed trend damps them (so they lead the slow engines instead of hunting between idle and full), and they travel at most 12 % a second.
- **Terrain**: every runway has an approach corridor (the ground stays under a 2.4° slope rising away from the threshold and at most 1 500 ft over the field — 1 000 ft under the 2 500 ft the autopilot levels at before it meets the glide path, `APPROACH_FLOOR_FT` — 32 km out; the glideslope is 3°) and a departure corridor (3.5° past the far end), so the final approach and the climb-out are always clear even at Bergen or Tromsø. Flying by hand, **TERRAIN — PULL UP** sounds when the ground 25 s ahead is less than 120 m below the flight path (not on the localiser, where the ground is meant to come up).
- **T** makes the time run one step faster, **R** one step slower: ×1 → ×2 → ×4 → ×8 → ×16 → ×32 → ×64 → ×128 → ×256 → ×512, only in the air above 500 ft AGL (on the ground only while the first officer taxis in, up to ×4: see Taxi speed). With the autopilot engaged up to ×512 at any height and in any phase (`TIME_ACCEL_AP_MAX`: the climb, the cruise and the descent alike) — only the approach slows it down; flying by hand ×2 above 1 000 ft AGL, ×4 above 3 000 ft, ×8 above 6 000 ft, ×16 above 8 000 ft, ×32 above 9 000 ft and ×64 above 10 000 ft. When the conditions get stricter (the autopilot off, lower down) it steps down to the fastest allowed step and says so; T at the limit says what would allow more. Outside a steady cruise (which is extrapolated, see below) the physics flies at most `MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME` steps a frame, so ×256 and ×512 in a climb or a descent run as fast as the device allows. Closing on the arrival the time slows down by itself (`Flight.approachAccelMax`, `Flight.ladderMax`): one step every 3 real seconds (`TIME_ACCEL_SLOWDOWN_S`), timed on the ground speed so that it is back at ×1 2.7 nm (5 km) out (`TIME_ACCEL_X1_NM`): at every step the distance left holds 3 s at every step below it. The distance for all these steps is the distance to the threshold divided by the cosine of the angle between the track and the way to it, at most ×5 (`Flight.accelDistNm`, `TIME_ACCEL_MIN_COS` 0.2): flown straight at the runway it is the distance itself, flown past or around the airport (or away from it) it is longer, so the time does not drop to ×1 there; each step says "TIME x64 — approaching ARN", and T inside that range says the time slows down by itself. The descent starts at the top of descent: at least 45 nm out, 3.2 nm per 1 000 ft to lose plus 12 nm (`Flight.descentStartNm`); long legs cruise up to 92 % of the type's cruise altitude. At 250 m/s over the ground (a jet in the cruise):

  | step | flown in its 3 s | down to the next step |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | ×512 | 384 km | 383 km (207 nm) before the 5 km point |
  | ×256 | 192 km | 191 km (103 nm) before it |
  | ×128 | 96 km | 95 km (51 nm) before it, and so on down to ×1 there |

  For a turboprop at 150 m/s every distance is 0.6 times as long. A whole 3 400 nm leg at ×512 takes about a minute of real time.

  **Interruptions** (`Flight.interruptTime`, `Flight.timeHeld`): an emergency, and ice building with the anti-ice off (the "Ice building — engine anti-ice K" warning, `Systems.iceHold`: in icing conditions with the ice past 15 %), cut the time to ×1 at once and remember the step it ran at. While the checklist is open or the ice is still building with the anti-ice off, T is refused ("Work the checklist first — time runs at x1" / "Anti-ice first (K) — time runs at x1") without forgetting that step; once it is dealt with (the checklist closed; the anti-ice on, or out of the icing) the time climbs back to it a step a second (`TIME_ACCEL_RESUME_S`), as far as the limits of the moment allow. R forgets it: the pilot sets the time.

  **The cruise at ×256 and ×512 is extrapolated** (`Flight.coast`): flying every 1/60 s step there would be 15 000–30 000 physics steps a second. When the flight is steady (`Flight.steadyCruise`: CRUISE on the autopilot's NAV, no checklist, no speed brake or windshear, the terrain not holding the autopilot up, within 2.5 m/s of level, 60 m of the selected altitude, 4° of wings level, 3° of the NAV heading and 10 kt of its speed — `SIM.COAST_*`), each frame the physics flies only 32 × the real time (`COAST_PHYS_ACCEL`: half a second of flight a frame at 60 fps, enough for the autopilot, the attitude and the instruments to stay alive) and the rest is dead reckoning in 1 s steps (`COAST_STEP_S`): the height and the true airspeed held, the heading turned towards the NAV heading at most 1°/s (`COAST_TURN_DEG_S`, the autopilot's gentle corrections), the steady wind of each point added (the drift is the autopilot's own), the fuel burnt at the present flow, the clocks advanced. The physics that follows in the same frame finds the aeroplane trimmed where it was, only further on, so the autopilot has nothing to correct and the picture does not jump; whatever is not steady (a turn, a climb for the terrain, an emergency) is flown by the physics, as fast as `MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME` allows. A cruise at ×512 burns the same fuel and arrives at the same moment as one flown step by step at ×128 (a headless 3 400 nm leg: 515 min, 9 448 against 9 443 kg of fuel), for a fifteenth of the work. The turbulence fades out above ×8 (`TURB_FULL_ACCEL`: its strength × 8 / the time step) — seconds of gusts in a frame only shook the picture and made the autopilot chase them; the near terrain is built in cells as big as a frame's flight needs (`Scene3D.updateNearTerrain`, a power of two × 420 m), and no airport on the way is built while the time runs at ×256 or more (it would be gone again in a second). It drops back to ×1 on any emergency (a checklist runs at ×1) and on the ground; once the checklist is closed (worked or not) it climbs back to the step it was at, one step a second (`TIME_ACCEL_RESUME_S`, `Flight.timeAccelResume`), as far as the conditions allow — T or R stops that. A message about the time or the autopilot replaces the one before it on the same topic (`info(text, topic)`; a warning's topic is its id), so pressing T five times leaves one "TIME x32", not five. The simulation runs at a fixed 60 Hz on a real-time accumulator, so the speed is the same at any frame rate.

**Trim.** The pitch axis has static stability towards a trim angle of attack, and the trim follows the angle of attack being flown (1.5°/s with the autopilot, 0.6°/s by hand, never past 2° below the stall warning) — like an automatic trim, so a heavy jet flying slowly is not fighting its own stability. Except by hand on the approach below 150 m AGL (`SIM.FLARE_TRIM_HOLD_M`): there the trim stays where the approach left it, so a pull in the flare is given back when the stick is let go and the nose comes down to the glide path again — otherwise a pull at 30 m held the nose up, the aeroplane floated past the runway and stalled.

## Emergencies (the point of the game)

Every flight draws its emergencies from the weighted list in `data/emergencies.js` (Easy: one, Medium: one or two, Hard: two), each pinned to one of its phases and a random moment in it (the gear failure only on aircraft with retractable gear, the load shift only on a cargo contract, the trouble with passengers — `paxOnly` — never on one). An emergency **interrupts the flight**: time acceleration is cut to 1×, the master caution lights, a caution chime sounds, and the **QRH checklist** panel opens:

- the title, a timer bar, and one line on **what happened** ("Fire warning on engine 2: the bell is ringing and its EGT is climbing.") — with the real engine number;
- the steps **top to bottom, in QRH order**, each with the **control that works it** on the right: the current step is lit, done steps are ticked;
- every step is worked with a real control of the aeroplane, or, for the switches that only exist in the QRH (a fire handle, a crossfeed valve, a call to ATC), with **Enter** / the **Go** button / a tap on the lit step. The checklist moves on by itself as soon as the control has been worked (a step stays ticked even if the autothrottle then moves the levers):
  - `switch` — Enter / Go / tap; `setAlt` / `setAltBy` — Enter sets the autopilot altitude (an absolute value, or so many feet below the present one; never below the arrival field + 600 ft) and engages the autopilot;
  - `idle` — the thrust levers to idle (**0**, the throttle slider down); `thrustMax` / `thrustMin` — the thrust at most / at least a value (the digit keys, the slider);
  - `antiIce` (**K** / Ice), `gearDown` (**G** / Gear, even though the gear does not move), `spoiler` (**/** / Spoiler), `apOff` (**Y** / AP), `parkBrake` (**Space** / Park);
  - `climb` — climbing at 500 fpm or more (pull the nose up); `slowVne` — the speed back below Vne;
- Enter or a tap on a step that is done with a control (or on a later step) does nothing but say which control it wants ("This one is done with the controls: press 0 — thrust to idle");
- **checklist hints** (Easy, or the Advanced Systems course) add one line under the lit step on **why** it is done;
- a few steps can go either way: a fire may still burn after the first bottle (30 %: one more step, "FIRE STILL ON — fire bottle 2"), a failed engine may **relight** (40 %: it runs its start sequence again), and the alternate gear extension drops the gear on its own weight;
- when the checklist is done, the panel turns green for a few seconds with the time used ("complete in 14 s of 25 s") and what the crew now knows ("Fire out. Engine 2 is shut down — set the thrust again and fly on with the other one."); run out of time and it turns red with what went wrong, and the failure escalates (the penalty: damage, lost fuel, lost reputation or pay, plus the specific consequence — a lost engine, a gear that drops hard, …).

The checklist clock is real time; its length is the emergency's limit × the course bonuses × the difficulty factor. The result card (green: done, red: lost) stays `QRH.OUTCOME_SEC` seconds, or goes at once with a click or a tap on it (a × in its corner says so; `HUD.outcomeGone`). The tuning (the chances, how long a ticked step stays, how long the result stays) is `QRH` in `constants.js`.

What the emergencies actually do to the aeroplane: an engine fire or failure removes that engine's thrust, a fuel leak drains 40–80 % of the tank capacity per hour, hydraulic failure weakens the brakes, depressurisation raises the cabin altitude, the gear failure locks the gear up until the checklist is done, a nav failure hides the route guidance, a bird strike limits one engine to 70 % N1, icing keeps building ice (lift down, drag and stall speed up) until anti-ice is on, windshear is a 14 s downdraft with a loss of headwind, a load shift pitches the nose down, an overspeed is a gust that pushes the speed 8 kt past Vne. The cabin and the ground trouble (a lithium battery fire, an unruly passenger, lavatory smoke, a laser attack, a passenger in labour) change nothing on the aeroplane: they cost time and attention, and mishandled they cost reputation and pay (a diversion), a little damage (two extinguishers in the lavatory, a hard landing half blinded by the laser).

**Who meets the aeroplane.** An emergency's `meet` (`ambulance`, `fire`, `police`) names who its `done` text calls to the gate; once its checklist is worked they are added to `Flight.meet` (`Systems.finishChecklist`; a checklist that ran out of time calls nobody) and `render/responders3d.js` puts them at the arrival stand (`Scene3D.setFlightGates(gates, arrival)`), into the arrival airport's frame: an ambulance (a white box van, yellow and green checks, red crosses), an airport fire engine (a red crash tender with a water cannon on the roof) and a police car (white, blue and yellow checks, a light bar). They stand along the fuselage facing out to the apron — the ambulance on the left (the front door's side), the police car further out beyond it, the fire engine on the right — each as near the front door as the wing and the engines of the type flown allow (`modelLayout`: its back 1.5 m ahead of the leading edge, an engine's intake or a propeller in its lane), its front at most 19 m past the stand, clear of the service road; where that does not fit (a big jet's wing) it stands 2 m further out to the side, again and again. Their blue lights flash in double flashes, side to side, each vehicle on its own beat, a glow round each lamp that grows at night. Engine fire → the fire service; medical emergency and baby on the way → an ambulance; lithium battery fire → the fire service and an ambulance; unruly passenger, lavatory smoke and laser attack → the police.

## Difficulty

Chosen on the title screen and changeable in settings; applies to every flight of the career.

Also offered in the pause, debrief and failure dialogs (pre-selected); a change applies from the next flight or the restart. The level belongs to the career: it is kept in its save (`Career.data.difficulty`, `Career.setDifficulty`), like the departure time and the start at the gate, so it stays what the pilot picked whatever happens in the browser's settings. The last pick (`settings.difficulty`) is only the level a new career starts at — the new career screen sets that one and leaves the career being left alone; an older save without a level takes it. A screen that has just come up over the flight (the debrief, a failure, the pause) ignores the arrows, Space and Enter for its first 0.7 s (`UI_KEY_LOCK_MS`), and every screen ignores a held key's repeats, so the arrows and the parking brake still pressed from the flight never wander onto a difficulty chip and press it.

- **Easy** — wind ×0.5, turbulence light, one emergency per flight, checklist hints (why each step) and ×1.5 time, generous landing grading. Taxiing is by hand on every level; only the first officer, earned with 5 flights in the type, taxis in (see The first officer taxis in).
- **Medium** — wind ×1.0, moderate turbulence, one or two emergencies, checklists without hints and with normal time, standard grading.
- **Hard** — wind ×1.5, severe turbulence, two emergencies every flight, ×0.75 checklist time, strict grading, ×1.5 damage from mishandled emergencies.

Every aircraft is drawn by `models.js` from its dimensions and its `look` (low or high wing and its dihedral, engines under the wings / on the rear fuselage / turboprops and the size of their fans or the number of propeller blades, T-tail, winglets, hump, a tall body and a double deck, fixed gear, rows of wheels on the bogies, base and accent colours): a fuselage of cross-sections with a drooping nose and a tail cone whose top line stays level while the belly sweeps up, narrowing to its tip (no thin tube at the end), with the fin's root inside it all along (`look.tall`: each section that many times as tall as it is wide, grown upwards from the same belly — the A380 1.18 with `decks: 2`, two rows of windows and the cockpit between them, the An-124 1.1; the 747's upper deck a hump over the front third), a canvas livery (windows, cheatline, cockpit glass, cargo door on freighters; like the fin's emblem and the fan faces it goes to the GPU as plain bytes in linear light, `paintedTexture` — some phones drew canvas textures black in the title's and the hangar's off-screen renderer), tapered swept wings with dihedral (`look.dihedral` overrides it: the 787's wing bent up 7°, the An-124's drooping −3°), fin and tailplane (a T-tail's tailplane spans 0.15 of the wing span each side, a low one 0.17–0.19, as on the real ones), nacelles with fans and exhaust cones (turboprop nacelles with a tail cone; a big fan that would come within 15 % of its diameter of the ground is pulled up level with the wing and forwards out of it, as on the 777 and the A350), spinning propellers (four blades, six on the ATR 72-600; blurred into a disc at speed), and gear with bogies on the big jets (two rows of wheels; three on the 777's main gear and the A380's body gear, five on the An-124's, the leg in the middle of a longer bogie; the 747 and the A380 have body gear between the wing gear, the high-winged An-124 does not). The fuselage's triangles face outwards (it is wound from the top towards the right side), and so does every other part: the lofted surfaces are closed at the root and the tip, and the open tube of a jet nacelle has a dark inside (its back faces) and a fan face drawn from both sides, so an engine seen from behind is not half see-through. From the front a jet engine shows its fan: a polished inlet lining behind the lip, 22 swept blades catching the light round a dark hub in the dark casing (a canvas texture, lit a little from inside so they read in the cowling's shadow), and a spinner with a white spiral; the fan turns with the power (`userData.fans`, slowly enough never to strobe backwards). A low wing has a centre section through the body, the root's section from one root to the other, so that where the body is narrower than the roots at the wing's height (the A380's double-deck belly) there is no gap between the wing and the body seen from below. The wings, the tailplane and the fin are lofted lifting surfaces (`liftingSurface`): a NACA-like section, a little flatter underneath on the wing, thinning from root to tip, with a rounded tip. Behind the hinge line (72–76 % of the chord) they are cut into pieces, and the moving ones hang from pivots on the real hinge line: **flaps** (inner 60 % of the span, down to 35° at full flap), **ailerons** (to 20°, opposite on the two wings), **spoilers** (panels ahead of the flaps, raised 50° with the spoiler), **elevators** (to 23°) and the **rudder** (to 23°), all following the controls. The **gear** folds up about the top of each leg as it travels — the nose gear forwards, a jet's main gear inwards into the belly, a low-wing turboprop's main gear (under the engine nacelles) forwards — and disappears when it is up. In an airline's colours (`build(ac, { airline })`) the canvas also gets the belly, nose, cheatlines, the fin colour running into the tail cone and the titles on both sides (drawn upside down and backwards on the right side, where the canvas runs the other way), and the fin gets two transparent decals clipped to its outline with the airline's fin art, each drawn to read the right way round. The Beluga has a hold of its own (`look.bubble`: its width over the A300's fuselage under it; `bubbleSection` in `sim/airframe.js`, `bubbleRing` / `bubbleGeometry` in `render/models.js`): a second body of elliptic sections over the lower fuselage — in the middle a circle meeting the fuselage in a crease at the cargo floor, at the front a blunt forehead riding on the cockpit roof (its door, the seam painted round it) before it comes down the sides, at the back a roof sinking under the fin, which stands on it 1.3 m higher than the A300's — with a canvas of its own in the operator's paint (the tail colour running up into the fin, the titles big on its sides; `bubbleLivery`), end plates near the tips of the tailplane (`look.tailFins`) and bogies under a span below 50 m (`look.mainRows`); the collision prism and spheres take the hold's width and roof (`lay.halfW`, `lay.top`). The An-225 has shapes of its own (`modelLayout`, which `build` places every part by and the Mriya's blueprint draws): six engines under the wing (`look.engines: 'wing6'`, at 0.2, 0.4 and 0.6 of the half span), a twin tail (`look.tail: 'twin'`: the tailplane on the roof of the tail, 0.185 of the span each side, a fin with its rudder at each end of it, a third of the fin below), the main gear in long sponsons along the belly (`look.sponsons`) and seven rows of wheels; and its finish (`look.finish`; `build(ac, { finish })` with an id of `MRIYA_FINISHES`, its own one without): every surface a shining Phong material of the finish's shine (`specular`, `shininess`: satin for Obsidian, a mirror for Platinum), the fins and the engine lips in its accent, the livery canvas lighter on the roof (`light`), deeper under the belly (`shade`), its lines along both sides (one under the other, or one stripe flowing through their colours) and AN-225 MRIYA in its title colour — never in an airline's paint (the contract's client is on the strip, not on the aeroplane). The main parts of every model are tagged (`g.userData.tags`: fuselage, wing, fairing, fin, tailplane, nacelle, pylon, gearNose, gearMain, sponson) for the assembly hall. The same models stand at the other gates in the home airlines' colours — airliners (up to the 787 and the 777) at the big airports, narrowbodies, regional jets and the ATR at the medium ones, regional types and turboprops at the small ones. ## The Mriya's assembly hall

The legend of the game: the **Antonov An-225 Mriya** ("dream" in Ukrainian), built in Kyiv in 1988 to carry the Buran shuttle on its back, the heaviest aeroplane ever flown, one ever finished, burned at Hostomel in February 2022. It cannot be leased: the pilot builds it again from **50 parts** (`MRIYA_PARTS` in `data/mriya.js`), and it is theirs. The hall (`ui/mriya.js`) opens from the legend's card in the hangar; its parts and its colours are sold at any time, to anyone with the money (and, for a part, the reputation). **Flying** it takes **every course** passed (`Career.mriyaOpen`: all of `COURSES`; `Career.unlocked` of the legend is built and every course): until then the hall and the hangar card say so and count the courses passed, and the built aeroplane's card has no Select (passing the last course makes a Mriya already built new in the hangar).

- **The price.** Each part costs money and the reputation of one client group (passenger airlines, cargo carriers or bush & SAR operators — they vouch for the pilot at the Antonov works): its share of `MRIYA.PRICE_KR` (10 000 000 kr: `kr` of the part over the sum of every part's `kr`) and of `MRIYA.PRICE_REP` (100: `rep` of the part over the sum over its group's parts), rounded to `ROUND_KR` (1 000 kr) and `ROUND_REP` (0.5) — `Career.mriyaPrice`. Changing a total re-prices everything. Each group's `rep` add up to exactly 100, so a part's `rep` is its price in reputation. The cheapest six parts of each group (in kronor) are a ladder to start on early: 0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 reputation (the radome, the air conditioning and the electrics; the roof cranes, the fuel system and the rudders; the wingtips, the emergency kit and the anti-ice); the rest share the remaining 94 by a blend of their weight and their kronor price, up to 13 for the centre fuselage; from the wingtips at 22 000 kr up to the centre wing at 566 000 kr. 17 parts cost passenger, 16 cargo and 17 bush reputation, 100 of each in all, and 10 000 000 kr together, as before. Buying takes the money and the reputation (`Career.buyMriyaPart`; never below zero, a part the pilot cannot pay for in either says what it needs).

## Controls

Keyboard:

- **↑ / ↓** or **W / S** — pitch (elevator). **Nose up** on the title screen and in the pause (`settings.noseUp`): **↓ Down** (the default — ↓, S and the touch stick pulled down lift the nose, like pulling back a yoke) or **↑ Up** (↑, W and the stick pushed up lift it; `Input.invertPitch` flips the pitch axis). The hints follow the choice: How to fly, the take-off prompt, the climb step of a checklist (↑ / stick ▲) and the touch names of the keys.
- **← / →** or **A / D** — roll (aileron)
- **Q / E** — rudder (ground steering on the nosewheel)
- **Z / X** — throttle down / up (also **− / +**, **PageDown / PageUp**)
- **1 … 8** — throttle to 10 % … 80 % instantly · **9** — full power (the take-off) · **0** — idle
- **Enter** — the next ground step: push back, start the engines, take-off clearance
- **G** — landing gear (locked only with weight on wheels; above Vlo + 25 kt a gear overspeed warning) · **F** — flaps extend one notch (always; above Vfe + 10 kt a flap overspeed warning and damage) · **V** — flaps retract · **B** — wheel brakes (hold) · **Space** — parking brake (releasing it with the engines running starts the taxi) · **/** — spoiler: a speed brake in the air (more drag, less lift); after touchdown it puts the weight on the wheels, so the wheel brakes are 35 % stronger with it out (`SIM.SPOILER_BRAKE_GAIN`). The prompts say when: high on the descent (more than 2 500 ft over a 3° profile) "spoiler to come down faster", back on the profile "spoiler in", fast on the approach (Vref + 40 within 12 nm) "idle and spoiler", out on the approach "spoiler in before the landing" (a warning below 150 m), out on the rollout "idle, spoiler, brakes", and off the runway "spoiler in" · **K** — engine and wing anti-ice
- **← / →** also steer the nosewheel on the ground (the rudder keys do too)
- **Y** — autopilot · **N** — back to the programme: NAV along the route and the altitude the flight plan wants now (the cruise level, or 2 500 ft above the arrival after the top of descent); engages the autopilot if it is off · **, / .** — selected altitude down / up · **; / '** — selected heading (HDG mode)
- **T / R** — time faster / slower (above 500 ft AGL: up to ×512 with the autopilot in CMD at any height, slowing down by itself on the approach; by hand ×2 / ×4 / ×8 / ×16 / ×32 / ×64 above 1 000 / 3 000 / 6 000 / 8 000 / 9 000 / 10 000 ft) · **M** — moving map (north up; on a large desktop window it cycles mini map → big map → off) · **C** — the next view, **X** (or **Shift+C**) — the previous one, **Z** — straight back to the cockpit (see Views) · **I** — the instrument lights, in turn dim, medium, bright (`CONTROLS.INSTRUMENT_LIGHTS`: 30 / 60 / 100 % of the light, the panel darkened by the rest × `INSTRUMENT_DIM_DARK`; a flight starts bright) and the instruments hidden: no panel and no heading strip, the view goes down to the bottom of the screen (`Cockpit.panelTop` is the screen's bottom, the outside views stop aiming above the panel); the HUD, the ILS, the approach path and the flight path marker stay; the next press brings them back dim · **H** — controls card · **Esc** — pause
- The keys are read by their place on the keyboard (`e.code`), not by the character they type, so the controls work in any layout (Russian, Swedish, …).
- The cheats use **Alt + digit** so they cannot fire in normal play.
- Overlay buttons (title, ops, briefing, debrief, pause) are pressed with Space / Enter, the arrow keys move between them (a button in the same row or column always wins over a nearer one off to the side, so → from Continue career goes to New career, not down to the difficulty chips). A press draws the screen anew, but the focus stays on the same button (a tab, a chip, a card's button — the selected type keeps its **✓ Selected** button for this) and, on the same screen, so does the scroll; a button that is gone leaves the focus on the screen's default or on the button now in its place (`UI.keepPlace`). **Esc** presses the screen's way back (the button marked `data-esc`: ← Menu in the hub, ← Back to the board in the briefing, ← Back on the new career and the pause's cards, Keep it, Got it); in the pause menu itself it resumes.
- A route is written with a drawn arrow between its ends — ARN, a contrail fading in behind a small aeroplane, LED (`routeHtml` / `routeArrow` in `ui/hud.js`, sized by the text, the same on every phone instead of a font's thin → character): on the offers (the codes, the cities, the stands), the briefing, the contract strip, the pause, the debrief, the flight's opening caption and the log.
- The buttons say what they are: the screen's main action is filled gold (Fly it, Start flying, Next flight, Resume), a card's action filled blue (Fly this, Select, Buy the rights, a course), both with → when they lead on; the way back is a plain outlined button with ←, at the top left of a sub-screen (on the new career, on the left of the row with the main action on the right).

Touch (phones/tablets): the left half is a floating joystick (pitch/roll and nosewheel steering on the ground; its ring appears where the finger lands), the right edge of the panel has a vertical throttle slider, as tall as the panel and off the view (a thrust lever: thrust = position^1.8, so the lower half gives fine control of taxi power; the knob shows the thrust in % and follows the autothrottle and the keys when the thumb is off it), and the buttons in four columns and three rows: **Menu · View · Map · Ice** (anti-ice), **Flap − · Flap + · Gear · Spoiler**, and a row that changes with the flight (`HUD.updateButtons`, `#touchButtons[data-show]`): on the ground **Go** (the Enter step) **· Brake** (toggle) **· Park**; after the lift-off nothing (the row keeps its place, so nothing jumps; a brake left on is let go), from 150 m (500 ft) up, where the climb starts (`CONTROLS.TOUCH_AP_ROW_AGL_M`), **AP · NAV** (back to the programme) **· Time − · Time +**; when the autopilot lets go on the approach (at the decision height, or switched off) Go and the brakes again, for the landing (a go-around brings the autopilot back). The stick's ring shows only under the thumb. The switches that stay set light up on their buttons: AP, NAV with the autopilot on and the gear down in green, the spoiler out and the parking brake set in red. On a phone on its side the buttons sit in the top right (leaving room for the messages between them and the contract strip); the throttle slider stands on the right edge of the panel, 10 px from its top and the bottom, on a tablet too (where the configuration block moves left of it); held upright (`Cockpit.portrait`: a coarse pointer and the screen taller than wide) they run across the top, the prompt sits under them on the left (54 % of the width), the messages on the right, the QRH checklist under them across the screen, the ILS scales in the bottom right corner of the view, and the throttle slider sits under the panel's configuration line; the view keeps more than half of the screen. On a phone on its side the contract strip is folded to the route (a ▸ after the route; a tap opens it, another folds it), so the prompt under it stays off the panel; held upright there is no strip at all (the route and the contract are in the pause menu, The flight). The fuel shows on a phone only when it is short (`HUD.fuelShort`): leaking, the low-fuel light, or less on board than 1.05 × what the rest of the route takes as the contract planned it (`CONTROLS.FUEL_SHOW_MARGIN`; a flight starts with at least 1.15 ×) — then a red FUEL line in the folded strip, held upright on its own. A failed flight's debrief shows LOST in a smaller type, so it fits a phone. `HUD.render` keeps the CSS variables `--buttonsW`, `--buttonsH` and `--panelH` up to date for this. In a QRH checklist the lit switch is tapped (or Go); the other steps are worked with the real controls. All multi-touch, so you can fly and work a checklist at the same time. Fullscreen is requested by the Continue / Start flying / Fly it buttons on coarse-pointer devices.

Mouse cursor: in fullscreen it hides once the mouse has been still for `CURSOR_HIDE_MS` (3 s) and comes back as soon as the mouse moves, clicks or scrolls (`core/cursor.js`).

Updates: a page left open (above all the installed Android app, resumed from the background) looks for a new deploy on every return to the foreground and once an hour, and reloads into it only on the title or the operations screen — never from the briefing to the debrief of a flight (`core/update.js`).

## Views

**C** / **X** (or **Shift+C**) (touch: **View**) cycle through `VIEW.MODES` forwards / backwards, **Z** jumps straight to the cockpit (`scene3d.js` `placeCamera`); the views riding with the aeroplane are in its own axes, scaled to its length, span and fuselage:

- **Cockpit** — the pilot's eye, with the cockpit frame and the windshield effects. You sit in the captain's (left) seat, 0.14 of the fuselage diameter left of the centreline (`VIEW.COCKPIT_SEAT_X`), looking straight ahead, so the centre window post is not in front of the nose but off to the right, where the geometry puts it (`Cockpit.postX`: about 30° right of the eye, `VIEW.CENTRE_POST_AHEAD`), leaning in at the top.
- **Chase** — behind and above.
- **Front, looking back** — high in front of the nose, looking back over the whole aeroplane. Like the chase and the wing views it never stands inside a building: by a terminal (at a stand the front view would be in it) the eye is pulled in along its line towards the aeroplane until it is `VIEW.BUILDING_CLEAR_M` (3 m) clear of every building, a terminal counted up to its roof and its lip over the apron (`Scene3D.clearOfBuildings`, `Collide.nearBuilding`).
- **Wing** — off the left wingtip, looking forward.
- **Tail fin** — on top of the fin, looking forward along the fuselage.
- **Landing gear** — under the nose, looking back at the main gear and the runway (a landing from here is spectacular).
- **Top down** — straight down from above, nose up.
- **Straight down** — a camera under the belly looking straight down at the ground, nose up the screen (`VIEW.MODES` 'down').
- **Tower / fly-by** — a sequence of shots, each held 3–5 s of real time whatever the time acceleration (`Scene3D.towerShot`, `VIEW.FLYBY`), so the picture never jumps about at high speed: near an airport (14 km) from above the nearest tower's cab for as long as the aeroplane is in range; en route in turn a pass (the camera waits beside the flight path ahead, placed by how far the aeroplane flies in the shot — its speed times the time acceleration — so it passes halfway through), a lead (ahead of the aeroplane, moving with it, looking back), from far off to one side (the aeroplane slowly overtaking it) and a trail (behind it, rising a little); when a pass would be more than 3 km away (the time running fast) only the moving shots. The moving shots follow the track turned slowly, so a turn does not swing them round. It zooms (the field of view follows the distance) so the aeroplane stays the same size.

**The cockpit inset.** On the ground, in any view but the cockpit, the pilot's view is drawn in a frame where the mini map goes (a large desktop window; on a tablet under the buttons; not on a phone), so the aeroplane can be taxied watched from outside without losing the way ahead: `HUD.updatePip` shows the frame and hands its rectangle to `Scene3D.renderPip`, which draws the scene again into that rectangle of the canvas (scissor) through a camera at the pilot's eye (`Scene3D.cockpitPose`, the cockpit view's), the own aeroplane hidden. It goes when the aeroplane lifts off (the mini map comes back if it is on) and while the big map, the help or a checklist is open. The taxi guidance arrow is over the windshield in the cockpit and centred just above the heading strip in the views from outside, under the aeroplane, so it never covers it.

**The panel on a computer.** From the left: the engines (N1, EGT and FIRE / FAIL / START on each engine's bar, the fuel), the turn coordinator, the airspeed, the attitude (with the radio altitude), the altimeter, the VSI, and on the right the configuration block (`Instruments.config`: throttle, flaps, gear, brakes, spoiler, the autopilot mode and the time acceleration, anti-ice, ice) with four caution lights two by two under its lines (`Instruments.cautions`: FUEL — a leak or low fuel (under 6 % of the tanks, or half an hour of cruise fuel, whichever is less), HYD, CABIN, DAMAGE, amber when lit) — only what no other instrument shows: a fire and a failed engine are on the engine bars, the ice, the gear and the autopilot are lines of the block, a stall is the STALL warning on the view. In a low panel the lines close up so the lights fit; a small window on a computer has them too.

**The panel on a phone.** On its side it keeps the airspeed, the attitude (with the radio altitude), the altimeter and the VSI in a row, all four the same size, and a configuration block of four lines: flaps, gear, autopilot mode and time acceleration, ice (`Instruments.phoneConfig`); the throttle is on the slider, the brakes, spoiler, anti-ice and autopilot on their lit buttons, the next waypoint on the map. The right 78 px (`PHONE_THROTTLE_W`) are the slider's. Held upright the panel is taller and has the configuration on one line across the top and the airspeed, attitude, altimeter and VSI two by two under it, at 80 % of the size the width left of the slider allows (`PHONE_UPRIGHT_GAUGES`, `Instruments.uprightR`), the panel as tall as they need (about a third of the screen), and the window pillars are thin. A phone has no heading strip (the taxi arrow, the map and the route marker do its job). A small window on a computer keeps the compact layout with the VSI and the full configuration block.

**The camera's flights** (`render/cinematic.js`, `CINEMATIC` in `constants.js`). Every flight but a practice landing begins with a wide shot and the camera flying into the captain's seat (6.5 s): at the gate from behind and above the aeroplane, off to its left, the terminal ahead of it; at the runway the same, the runway across the picture ahead. It sweeps in behind the aeroplane, over the tail fin and along the roof, and drops into the seat; the last 0.65 s it is inside, the own aeroplane hidden, while the cockpit frame and the panel fade in. Parked at the arrival gate (`PARKED`, engines stopped) it flies the same way out to the right — out of the seat, over the fin, back and up to a wide shot of the aeroplane at its gate and the terminal (5.5 s), holds it 1.2 s — and the debrief follows; a pilot who parked watching from outside starts it at the roof, not in the seat. The path is a spline through five keys in the aeroplane's own axes, scaled to its length, span and fuselage (the wide shot 1.9 × the larger of the length and the span + 50 m out, 40° off the tail), the times of the keys part of the spline (a non-uniform Catmull-Rom, still at both ends), so it eases in and out without a jerk; the field of view opens from 50° to the cockpit's 68°. Meanwhile the aeroplane stands still (no simulation, no cabin announcement; the tower's clearance at the runway waits for the cockpit), the HUD is hidden, black letterbox bars slide in while the camera is outside, the intro fades in from black with the route under the picture (the cities with their flags, the client and the type) and the outro names the arrival and the gate; the sound is heard from outside, as in the outside views. Enter, Space, Esc or a tap anywhere skips it (the outro on the next frame, so the key does not also press a button of the debrief); a cheat ends the intro.

**The look round an airport** (`Cinematic.tour`, `Game.tour` / `startTour` / `tourFrame`, `TOUR` in `constants.js`), from the briefing's **Look around** buttons, free. The own aeroplane, in the client's paint, stands where the flight finds it there: at the departure stand (or at the holding point, the engines running, when the pilot starts at the runway) or parked at the arrival stand, the stand's parked aeroplane hidden; the weather and the local solar hour are the ones the flight meets there (the arrival's: the departure time + the planned flight time + the longitudes between), but in a clear spell (`TOUR.MIN_VIS_M` of visibility at least, the cloud base at least `TOUR.MIN_CLOUD_M` above the field). The camera flies a spline through eleven keys in the runway's own coordinates: in on the approach from 2.6 km out and 240 m up, low (25 m) down the runway on its left, a climbing turn to the right past the far end, over the terminal's roof (zoomed in) and the apron, down to the aeroplane and slowly round it from the runway side, then holds 2 s. Each piece takes as long as the camera needs at `TOUR.SPEED_MS` (210 m/s), the last two at least 5 and 7 s, so a long runway makes a longer flight (about 50 s); the tangents are the chords between the neighbouring keys (a long straight into a tight turn would otherwise overshoot below the ground). On a screen held upright the aeroplane is seen from further off (up to 2.2 ×), so it fits across. Nothing else runs: no simulation, no sound, no HUD and no touch controls (`body.touring`); the letterbox bars stay in, captions name the airport (flag, name, code, city), then the runway (its number, length and the elevation), then the aeroplane and where it stands. Enter, Space, Esc or a tap ends it sooner; either way the briefing is back (`Game.mode` `tour`, then `briefing`).

The instrument panel stays on screen in every view, so outside the cockpit the projection is shifted up (`setViewOffset`) to keep the aeroplane in the open part of the screen.

## Sound

`audio.js`, all synthesized. Outside a flight (the menus, the briefing, the debrief, a practice landing's result, the pause) it is silent but for the buttons' clicks and the Mriya's assembly hall (below): a flight that ends inside a frame (the practice landing) is not voiced again by the rest of that frame, the main loop silences a flight sound left over in any other mode, and the callouts still queued are cancelled. Two buses: the world (engines, wheels, airflow) goes through a lowpass that muffles it in the cockpit and opens outside, where the sound also fades with the distance from the camera and shifts in pitch as the aeroplane approaches or leaves (Doppler, from the rate of change of the distance); the cockpit bus (warnings, levers, the stick shaker) is never filtered.

- **Engines** — a jet's fan whine (two detuned tones in a resonant band at 260–2 960 Hz with N1), its roar (noise through a lowpass that opens with the power, louder behind), the core rumble and, above 78 % N1, the buzz-saw of the fan tips (louder in front); a turboprop's blade beat (4 blades) and a thin turbine whine. They follow N2 during the start, so the spool-up is heard.
- **Airflow** — with the airspeed, more with the gear, the flaps and the spoiler out.
- **Wheels** — a rolling rumble with the ground speed (rougher on grass), a bump every 12 m on the taxiways and the apron and every 30 m on the runway (the slab joints), the brakes (a hiss, and a squeal when nearly stopped).
- **Systems** — the hydraulic pumps while the gear or the flaps travel; the gear clunks down-and-locked and up-locked, the flaps stop with a soft clunk, the spoiler hisses out and in, the levers click, the parking brake sets with a puff of air.
- **Touchdown** — a tyre chirp per main gear and a thump as hard as the landing; a hard landing rattles.
- **The cabin's reaction** (`Flight.cabinMood` / `cabinReaction`, `core/crowd.js`, `CABIN_REACTION` in `constants.js`) — with passengers on board (a passenger contract; never cargo, mail or a practice landing), on the graded touchdown at the destination, by its sink rate: up to 100 fpm an **ovation** (the whole cabin claps, longer and louder, one to three whistles through the fingers — the first a two-part "wolf whistle" — and a few "woo!"s), up to 200 **applause** (most of the cabin, now and then a whistle), up to 320 **a few claps**, up to 450 **firm** (the overhead bins rattle, a murmur), up to 600 (`SIM.TOUCHDOWN_HARD_FPM`) **rough** (an "ooh!", cups and cans clinking in the galley, the cabin chattering), above it **hard** (a gasp and an "ah!", a few yelps, the bins and the galley crashing, a baby crying with more than 20 on board, a long nervous chatter). A bank past 5° makes it one worse, a touchdown off the paved surface hard. A bounce — down again within 6 s — gets a collective "whoa" and stops any clapping short. Each is also a line in the messages ("The passengers applaud", "A firm one — the overhead bins rattle"…), so it is seen with the sound off. Everything is synthesized: the claps are rendered into a stereo buffer, every person with their own hands (the pitch and the ring of the clap), their own pace (3–6 a second) and seat (left or right, nearer or further), joining within a moment and stopping one by one; the crowd heard grows with the square root of the passengers (3 to 42 clappers), a full cabin up to 4 dB louder; the voices are sawtooth "throats" through two vowel formants ("ooh", "ah", "woo"), the chatter syllables of changing vowels, the galley inharmonic struck partials. In the cockpit it is all muffled behind the door (a 3.2 kHz lowpass), outside the aeroplane faint.
- **Warnings** — the master caution, the warning tone, the stick shaker on a stall warning, a two-tone chime crossing 10 000 ft.
- **The Mriya's assembly hall** (`core/fanfare.js`, through the master gain like everything else): a soft till chime when a part is bought, a heavy clunk and a ring of metal when one snaps into its place, and for the fiftieth the finale — six engines spooling up, a brass fanfare (G C E rising, a C major chord, F, then a big G-C chord) with timpani, and nine fireworks whistling up, bursting and crackling.
- **Callouts** (spoken in the game's language and units, with a voice of that language — en-GB for English; where the browser has no Russian or Swedish voice they are said in English): "80 knots" (metric: "150 km/h", 80 kt rounded like every metric speed), "V one", "rotate" on the take-off roll and "positive rate" once climbing (Russian: «Рубеж», «Подъём», «Есть набор»); on the way down to land the radio heights 1 000, 500, 100, 50, 40, 30, 20, 10 ft and "minimums" at 200 ft — metric: 300, 150, 30, 15, 10, 5 m and "minimums" at 60 m (at ×1 time only). The numbers go to the voice as whole numbers in digits, so it reads them in its own language. They respect the sound setting.
- **Cabin announcements** (`ui/cabin.js`, on a passenger contract, not in a practice): the cabin chime (`cue('pa')`), then the voice (`Audio2.announce`) — taxiing out: welcome aboard the client's flight to the city, the planned flight time (`blockMin`), seat belts, seat backs upright, window shades open; 20 s into the cruise: the captain with the height (to 1 000 ft / 100 m), the ground speed, the air outside and the time to go; at the top of descent: the time to the landing and the temperature at the arrival; on the approach: seat belts, seat backs, tray tables stowed, window shades open; off the runway: welcome to the city, the local time (`utcOffset`), the temperature, and ahead of or behind the schedule — the airborne time (lift-off to touchdown) against `blockMin`, within `SIM.CABIN_ON_TIME_MIN` 3 min it is on schedule. The city is said in the voice's language (`aptCity`). Each once a flight, in the game's language (the hours, minutes and degrees with the right word for the number, `CABIN_WORDS`; English with an English voice), never while a checklist runs (it waits). A callout cuts an announcement off, so V1 or a radio height is never late. The voice gets it a sentence at a time (`announcementParts`, a long sentence cut at its commas, at most about 160 letters): Chrome's network voices drop or cut off a long utterance, and the utterances are kept referenced until said. Each also puts "📢 Cabin announcement" on the HUD, so one a browser cannot voice still shows. **Cabin announcements** On / Off on the title screen and in the pause (`settings.cabinPa`).

## Cheats

Keyboard only, `Alt` + digit, never bound to anything used in normal play, each with an on-screen banner, and any use marks the whole run as cheated (no money, no reputation, no records — but a landing at the arrival still moves the aeroplane there, and the board is dealt again from it, as after every arrival):

- **Alt+1** — full fuel tanks · **Alt+2** — emergencies disabled for this flight · **Alt+3** — jump to a 12 nm final for the arrival runway, on the glideslope, autopilot coupled · **Alt+4** — +10 000 kr · **Alt+5** — repair all damage · **Alt+6** — time acceleration ×1024 on the autopilot (in the air, while it may run at ×512, not yet slowing down for the approach: extrapolated in a steady cruise) · **Alt+0** — show the cheat list (not a cheat itself).
- In the Mriya's assembly hall its own (`MriyaScreen.cheat`, a toast at the bottom): **Alt+7** — every course passed and every part bought, free · **Alt+8** — every part but one fitted (the last one left on the stand, to see the finale) · **Alt+9** — the build starts again (no parts, the An-225 gone from the hangar; its finishes kept) · **Alt+0** — the list. Alt+7 and Alt+8 count as cheats in the career and mark the build (`mriya.cheated`: the log says "built again (with cheats)").

`node world-aviation/tools/approach-check.js [ID …] [--type B738] [--from 90] [--verbose]` loads the logic files into a Node `vm` (no rendering, no sound) and, for every airport or the ones named, builds a flight's world to it and flies the real flight model and autopilot from 50 nm out on four sides, on the descent profile at 250 kt, the flaps and the gear as the prompts ask. Per approach it reports where the glideslope was captured, how high above it the aeroplane was 8 nm out, the clearance over the ground before it, whether the ground rose over the approach corridor on it, the terrain holds, and where and why the autopilot let go (it should be at 200 ft on the glide path); per airport how far the natural ground round the field is off its elevation and how much the corridors had to cut (more than 1 500 / 2 000 ft: the map data is off there). `--map ID` draws the ground round an airport as text. Run it after a change to the terrain, the map data or the autopilot; the four sides of all the airports take a few minutes.

`node world-aviation/tools/collide-check.js [ID …] [--type A225,A388] [--step 2]` lays every airport (or the ones named) out as a flight does and drives every type (or the ones named) as a kinematic nosewheel-steered pure pursuit of the guidance's carrot along every taxi route the game gives — from each stand's push-back point to the line-up, from each runway exit to each stand — then parks it on the stand, testing each pose against the buildings and the parked aeroplanes as `sim/collide.js` does (the stands in use free, `World.standsFor`). It prints every touch (the airport, the type, the route, what and how far along) and exits with 1 if there was one; run it after changing the layout, the parked types or an aircraft's dimensions. All 141 airports × 19 types at a 3 m step take about 20 minutes; a few airports or types, seconds.

`node --experimental-websocket tools/cdp.js world-aviation/tools/gfx-bench.js [out-dir] --limit 600` (from the repo root; `GFX_SCENES`, `GFX_TO=CPH`, `GFX_QUALITY` and the others in its header) measures what the picture costs on a phone held sideways: per scene (the departure's and the arrival's gate and apron by day and by night, the final at 2 and 0.5 nm, the rollout) the draw calls, the triangles, the points and the fill (fragments shaded per pixel: with the logarithmic depth buffer a phone shades every fragment, hidden or not), and what hiding each layer saves — the sky, the terrain, the clouds, the trees, the ground lights, the landing light, and the airport's parts by the builder that made them (`apt.parked`, `apt.buildings`, `apt.perimeter`, `apt.pools`, …). The scene `route` carries the aeroplane along the route and back and prints the GPU's geometries, textures and programs (a leak check). The milliseconds are this machine's GPU's; the counts are the phone's. `tools/gfx-probe.js` is the probe it injects; pasted into the console of a phone connected to `chrome://inspect` before a flight starts, `GfxProbe.census()` and `GfxProbe.measure()` give the same numbers on the phone itself.

## Language

Everything the player reads switches: the screens, the briefing and the debrief, the HUD strip, the prompts, the messages and warnings, the QRH checklists (titles, steps, the why, the results), the ILS words, the map hints, the touch buttons and the controls card (`data-i18n` in `index.html`), the aircraft, regions, client groups and payloads, the courses and the exams.

- The English text stays in the code and is the key: `tr('Taxi to {gate}', { gate })` (`core/i18n.js`) looks it up in `TEXT_RU` / `TEXT_SV` (`data/lang-ru.js`, `data/lang-sv.js`) and fills in the `{name}` values; a text with no entry is shown in English. Data tables (aircraft, regions, emergencies…) stay English and are passed through `tr()` where they are shown.
- The units stay as they are in every language (`ft`, `kt`, `nm`, `fpm`, `kg`), so `Units.text` still converts them, and so do the keys in `<kbd>` (on a touch screen the prompts and the messages swap them for the button names, in the same language: Space → Park, Y → AP, T → Time +, …; a message names its key in `<kbd>` too, never as a bare letter).
- The career log and the invoice keep the English template and its values (`logLine` in `career.js`), so they are shown in the language of the day; old saves show their English text.
- The airports and their cities are named in the game's language: `AIRPORT_NAMES` (`data/airport-names.js`: the Russian name and city of every airport, the Swedish ones where they differ — Göteborg, Köpenhamn, Helsingfors, Moskva, Peking, Kapstaden…) through `aptName(a, lang)` / `aptCity(a, lang)` in `core/i18n.js`, on the board, the briefing and its map, the pause, the HUD strip, the hangar's warnings and the camera's flights; the cabin announcements say the city in the voice's language (`Cabin.lang()`, English where the browser has no voice of the game's language), so a Russian voice says «Якутск», not a mangled "Yakutsk". The names painted at the airports stay in English, except at the airports of the game's own language: there the roof and the welcome banner are in Russian or Swedish (`roofLabel`, see the look of each airport).
- Stay English on purpose: the airline names, the instruments and caution light labels (as in a real cockpit) and the debug cheat banners.
- Adding a text: write it in English through `tr()`, add the same key to both tables. The check: every key used in the code and the data has an entry in both tables, with the same `{params}`.

## HUD layout

The view ahead stays clear for the landing. A left column (`#hudLeft`) holds the contract strip (the client and the load, the aircraft type (AIRCRAFT), the route with both airports' full names, flags and countries, the pay and the planned flight time at 1× (`blockMin`) — all fixed for the flight; then the live part: the actual flight time so far (`fl.elapsed`), the fuel, the weight now against the maximum take-off weight (WEIGHT, `fl.weight()`: empty + load + fuel, red above the MTOW), the time now at both ends of the route (LOCAL TIME, <departure> and LOCAL TIME, <arrival>: the departure's clock, running on with the flight, and the same moment on the arrival's clock — the two run together, apart by the difference of the two airports' UTC offsets, `utcOffset()`: the country's `tz` in `data/countries.js` or the airport's own `tz` in a big country with several zones, standard time without summer time), the outside air temperature at the aeroplane's height (the surface temperature of the departure, or of the arrival from the top of descent, minus `WEATHER.LAPSE_RATE` 6.5 °C per km above that airport)), under it the phase prompt and under that the messages (the last five, fading). A warning about a state (stall, overspeed, flap or gear overspeed, the gear lever locked on the ground, the spoiler out for the landing) leaves the screen the moment the state is put right. A take-off started at the holding point goes from the engine start straight to the holding point (flaps, then Enter for the clearance). On the approach the prompt says only the flap setting to select now: "flaps 3" — the furthest notch the speed allows (its Vfe + 5 kt), at least the next one. On a phone the messages leave the column: they sit at the top between the column and the buttons, the last three (the strip on a phone: see Touch). A tablet (a touch screen at least 600 px both ways) keeps them in the column under the prompt, the last five, as on a desktop. The ILS is on the right on a desktop (see Approach guidance), the QRH checklist on the right (on a phone on the left over the strip), the mini map in the top right corner.

## Units

`settings.units`: `aviation` (ft, kt, nm, fpm — the default) or `metric` (m, km/h, km, m/s), chosen on the title screen and in the pause. Everything inside the game stays in aviation units; `Units` in `utils.js` converts where things are shown: `Units.text` rewrites every "<number> ft|kt|nm|fpm" in prompts, messages, banners, checklists and the HTML screens (except inside an image's `data:` URL, whose base64 would otherwise be broken by a "3nm" in it); the instruments draw metric scales (airspeed in km/h, altimeter one turn = 1 000 m, VSI ±10 m/s), and each gauge's name and unit sit where its dial has no ticks (the altimeter's under the 0, the VSI's in the gap on the left, centred on the needle's axis); an airspeed number that would run into the digital window (the 1 000 km/h at the end of a jet's metric dial) is left out. Text built in code always carries its unit (`Units.alt` / `spd` / `dist`), never a bare number. Metric values are rounded to sensible numbers: altitudes to 10 m (50 m above 1 000 m), speeds to 5 km/h, distances to 1 km (0.1 km under 10 km), vertical speed to 0.1 m/s.

## The map

**M** opens the moving map (north up; on a touch screen the Map button opens it and a tap anywhere on it closes it, and it is scaled to fit a landscape phone): coastlines, airports, the planned route (dashed), and the **track actually flown** — `Flight.recordTrack` keeps a point every 60 m on the ground and every 600 m in the air (sooner on a turn), thins itself out past 2 400 points, and restarts after a jump (the final-approach cheat). Yellow on the ground, green in the air. The scale bar follows the units.

On a large desktop window (at least 1100 × 640, fine pointer) a **mini map** sits in the top right corner (the same map, without the other airports' names, with the distance to go instead of the scale bar, redrawn 6 times a second). It frames the whole route for the first half of the flight, then only the aeroplane, the arrival and its 12 nm final, so it zooms in as the arrival gets closer and the runway and its arrow stay readable; it glides between framings. It is shown only when it helps: in the air after the take-off phase, not during a QRH checklist (its result card, which stays a few seconds after it, moves down under the map while the map is up), not below 1 000 ft AGL on the approach, not while the big map or the controls card is open. **M** cycles mini → big → off → mini; the choice is remembered. Phones and small windows keep the big map on **M** only — and there the big map follows the flight like the mini map (the same framing and glide, `CONTROLS.MAP_GLIDE`), fills the screen at its pixel density and shows the distance to go next to the scale bar; on a large desktop window, with the mini map beside it, the big map keeps the whole route.

The arrival runway is drawn on both maps with its final approach: the extended centreline out to 12 nm (dashed magenta), an arrow down it in the landing direction, the runway itself and its number.

## Approach guidance

**Radio altitude.** The altimeter is barometric — it reads above the sea, like a real one, so over an airport 1 500 ft up it shows 1 500 ft on the runway. The height above the ground is the radio altitude, shown where the eye is on the approach: in a box under the aircraft symbol of the attitude indicator (like on a real PFD), labelled RA, whenever the aeroplane is in the air below 2 500 ft above the ground or the water right below it (`SIM.RADIO_ALT_MAX_FT`), in every view and with or without the landing aid. It is the height of the wheels, so it reads 0 at the touchdown, and it counts in finer steps close to the ground: 10 ft, 5 ft below 50 ft, 1 ft below 10 ft (metric: 5 m, 1 m below 20 m). On the way down to land it turns amber below the decision height, 200 ft (`SIM.DECISION_HEIGHT_FT`), together with the "minimums" callout.

Both are the **landing aid**: `settings.landingAid` (on by default), switched on the title screen and in the pause.


- **ILS** — on the approach (on the localiser), and on the descent within 23 nm (`SIM.ILS_AID_NM`) of the cosine distance (`Flight.accelDistNm`: the distance to the threshold over the cosine of the angle between the track and the way to it, at most × 5 — flying past the airport or away from it, it is far longer than the distance itself, so the scales do not come up early), two scales that read at a glance: the localiser (magenta, labelled RUNWAY) carries a little runway that sits where the runway is, the glideslope (cyan, labelled GLIDE PATH) a triangle that sits where the glide path is; the yellow marks in the middle are you. Under them a line of plain words says what to do — "runway to the RIGHT ▶ turn right", "HIGH ▼ descend more", or "ON THE CENTRELINE AND THE GLIDE PATH". Outlined text instead of a panel, so the view stays open. It is always at the right edge of the windscreen (`Cockpit.pillarW`; in the outside views of the screen), the glide path scale and its label 20 px from the window pillar (on a desktop on the left while a QRH checklist is open on the right): on a desktop at mid-height; on a tablet on its side (a touch screen at least 600 px both ways) under the buttons (`HUD.buttonsH`), held upright just above the heading strip; on a phone, either way round, in the bottom right corner of the view, as far from the pillar as from the glareshield (14 px), at three quarters of the size and without the labels and the words — the scales say it, and the view needs the room.
- **The approach path** — inside `SIM.GLIDE_AID_NM` (20 nm) of the runway, fading in over its last 5 nm (in the cruise and the descent by the cosine distance as for the ILS, on the localiser by the distance itself) a magenta ring hangs on the extended centreline at the height of the 3° glide path every nautical mile out to 12 nm, joined by a thin line, with the distance every 4 nm and a white marker at the threshold with the runway's name on a dark tag ("Runway 21", in the game's language). The rings are projected through whichever camera is in use, so they work from the cockpit and from every outside view: flying down the line of rings is flying the ILS. They are see-through (a faint fill in a bright rim), so the airport shows through them; they grow as they come closer (1.8–6.5 px), and where they crowd together far out the nearest are kept and the ones that would overlap them left out, as is a distance that would run into another label. ## Instruments

The airspeed dial starts at zero (so the needle never rests on a number it is not showing), with a number every 40–100 kt depending on the size, and the digital speed in a window; over the centre the true airspeed (**TAS**, in the same unit): the needle, the window and the autopilot's speed bug are the indicated airspeed, which high up reads far below the true one (a 737-800 cruising at 453 kt TAS at 37 000 ft shows about 250 kt, 460 km/h), while the hangar's cruise speed is a true airspeed; the VSI has its ticks and numbers on the arc the needle swings on (±2 000 fpm or ±10 m/s at ±150°), so it reads true everywhere; all needles are pointed, their tips exactly on the value.

## Quality presets

Auto / Low / Medium / High (remembered), picked from the device and the measured frame rate: terrain cell size, draw distance, polygon budget, cloud and tree counts, the land cover's tile size, the roundness of the trees round the airports (`treeDetail`: their crowns 20 faces on Low and Medium, 80 on High), pixel-ratio cap, windshield rain. The FPS counter shows the preset in use, and how many steps the Auto quality has shed ("Medium −2").

**The Auto quality** (`Game.autoQuality`, `AUTO_QUALITY` in `constants.js`) starts at Medium on a phone, High elsewhere. A flight below 30 fps for 3 s sheds one step, the least missed first — the trees in the dark (`dark` > 0.6, the terrain's and the airports', black on black there), every other cloud, half the ground lights at night (the cities' half stays: the points are laid out cities first), then the pixel ratio to 0.88 and 0.8 of the preset's (never below 1) — each only hidden or thinned per frame (`Scene3D.setShed`), so a step costs no stutter; with all of them shed and still slow the preset goes down (built again). Above 50 fps for 20 s the last step comes back; one whose return brings the low frame rate back within 10 s stays shed for the session. The 2 s after an airport, the terrain or the preset was built (`Scene3D.builtAt`) do not count. The resolution is the last thing to go: before, the one way down was the whole preset, and on a phone Medium → Low dropped the pixel ratio from 1.25 to 1 at once, for good. Never drawn when they would not show: the apron's pools of floodlight by day (one baked mesh, hidden while `dark` is 0) and the stars by day.
